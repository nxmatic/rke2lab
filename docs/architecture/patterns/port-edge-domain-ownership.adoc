= Who owns the port — domain, port, and edge, told apart
:toc: left
:toclevels: 3

[NOTE]
====
*Status:* DESIGN (2026-06-22). A companion to
link:frontier-playability-model.adoc[the frontier-playability model]. That doc names the three axes
(playability / edge / direction) and the two edge species. This one answers the question that keeps
recurring when extracting a `<target>-edge`: *what tells an edge apart from a domain, and who owns the
port between them?* It exists because we twice mis-modelled it — proposing a `ssh-to-age-port` and a
`ssh-to-age-core` for a thing that has neither.
====

== The three roles in one sentence

The *domain* is what is enclosed; the *edge* is its face toward an outside; the *port* is the membrane
between them — *and the port is owned by the consumer that calls it, never by the edge.*

Each role has exactly one verb. Keeping the verbs straight is the whole model:

[cols="1,1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Role | Verb | What it is / owns

| *domain* (`-core`)
| *CALLS*
| Pure logic that reasons over the model and never leaves the system (remove every external system, it
  still compiles and computes). Owns its model types; *declares + calls* the ports it needs (`@Reference`).

| *port* (`-port`)
| *DECLARES*
| The shared interface of call + the pure exchange records. A *passive type*, exported so both sides
  share the same class. It is NOT resolved and carries no _resolution/service_ capability — no
  `@Component`. (It does carry one passive *discovery marker*, `type=seam` — see
  <<the-boot-face,the boot face>> — which the resolver ignores, being a namespace with no `Require`
  side.) It is the *address*, not the door. Owned by the consumer (lives in its `-port` module), never
  named after the edge.

| *edge* (`<target>-edge`)
| *PROVIDES*
| The adapter whose reason to exist is *contact* with one external target (remove the target, nothing is
  left). *Implements* a port and *provides the capability* that makes that port reachable — it is the
  door SCR resolves. Owns its low-level contact types; exposes NO port of its own.
|===

== Who is resolved — the port is the address, the edge is the door

This is the pivot the whole model turns on, and the one we kept fumbling:

[quote]
____
The *port is not resolved* — it only provides the interface of call (the shared type). What is resolved
is the wiring between a *consumer that requires* and an *edge that provides*, expressed *through* that
type. The port is the address; the edge is the door.
____

So a port that is merely exported (`Export-Package`) gives *type resolution only* — the contract's class,
nothing about who implements it (the `system-export = resolution-only` invariant). Type-sharing alone
finds no door: a consumer holding just the package would have to `new EdgeImpl()`, re-coupling to the
edge (anti-DIP). The door is found because the *edge publishes a capability* keyed on the port type, and
the consumer requires it.

[mermaid]
....

  edge["target-edge — the PROVIDER<br/>implements + provides the capability (@Component)"]
  world["external target"]
  core -->|"① DECLARES + CALLS"| port
  edge -->|"② IMPLEMENTS"| port
  core -.->|"③ SCR resolves the wiring, keyed on the port type"| edge
  edge -->|"④ CONTACTS"| world

Read it as: the consumer (`①`) and the edge (`②`) both point AT the port — the port points at nothing,
which is why it is pure and passive. The actual resolution (`③`) runs consumer↔edge, *keyed on the port
type* but not flowing through the port as an actor. The edge carries the behaviour (the capability); the
port carries only the type. *That* is what tells port from edge — not "one is pure and the other does
I/O", but "the port is the type, the edge is the capability that makes the type reachable".

=== Two worlds, two resolution regimes

Whether the edge must publish a capability *depends on the consumer's world*:

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Consumer world | How the door is found

| *OSGi world* (e.g. ssh-to-age's consumer)
| The edge MUST publish a capability, else no bundle finds it without a coupling `new`. Two strengths:
  *(a) service* — `@Component` provides `osgi.service` keyed on the port type, `@Reference` binds it;
  *runtime, soft* (no edge → component just stays unsatisfied). *(b) resolution capability* —
  `Provide-/Require-Capability` on a namespace; *resolve-time, hard* (no door → the framework refuses to
  wire). For a *system boundary*, (b) is the better fail-mode: "no door ⟹ resolution fails" beats
  "silently inactive". Precedent: the unitrepo handler edge (`osgi.extender=unitrepo.handler` + a
  `unitrepo.type` namespace).

| *host world* (e.g. pulumi-edge, dbus-edge)
| NO capability needed *today*. The host is the flat assembler — it does
  `new PulumiInterventionLedgerWriter(...)` explicitly in `DoctorAssembly`. There is no registry to
  resolve against, so the edge provides nothing; it is simply instantiated by the wiring. This is why
  `pulumi-edge` never had to declare a capability.
|===

=== The world is DERIVED from the consumer, not a property of the edge

A correction worth stating loudly: an edge's world *follows the world of whoever calls it*. ssh-to-age
is the first external edge in the OSGi world for one reason — its consumer
(`DefaultManifestSynthesisService`) is a `@Component`. pulumi/dbus are host because their callers
(`DoctorAssembly`, the readiness gates) are host (non-playability is only the *secondary* reason their
impl could not move even if a caller did). So "which world?" is never answered by looking at the edge
alone; look at who requires it.

=== The registry is the single source of resolution (target) — vs the mixed present

The *target* state: a consumer always obtains an edge through the OSGi registry — never through a hand
written `if (host) new … else @Reference`. How the impl *enters* the registry follows playability
(`@Component` SCR when playable; a host-published `registerService` when not), but the *resolution* the
consumer sees is uniform: it requires the port type, the registry supplies the door.

Honest present (2026-06-22): this is *not yet* uniform. Today `DoctorAssembly` still does
`new PulumiInterventionLedgerWriter(backendDir)` — a host `new`, not a registry lookup. So three regimes
coexist: OSGi consumer → `@Reference`; host consumer of an OSGi service → the R4 `awaitService` seam;
host consumer of a host edge → `new`. The single-registry picture above is the destination this chantier
moves toward, not a claim about the code as it stands. (Recording it as target, not fact, keeps us honest
per `model-substrate-alignment` — do not describe a substrate that does not exist yet.)

[NOTE]
====
*The boot trap (resolved — `refactor/osgi-boot-single-source`).* The `META-INF/bundles` embed topology
WAS hand-listed at ~8 sites with no single source — `OsgiRuntime` admitted it ("poms cannot call Java,
this is the authoritative registry during review"). It is now single-sourced by a *capability scan*: each
model bundle self-declares `Provide-Capability: io.nxmatic.rke2lab.embed; type=model` in its `bnd.bnd`,
and `OsgiRuntime.boot()` scans `META-INF/bundles`, reads each jar's manifest, and installs exactly the
carriers of that capability. The boot-stack jars (pax/scr/resolver) do not carry the marker, so they are
excluded *objectively* — not by a name filter, which would be a hand-list in disguise that slips at the
first rename. All the Java bundle-name literals (`bootingEmbedded("…jar")`, the builder's
`embeddedBundle(…)` verb, the tests' `embeddedBundle(…)`) are gone; adding an embedded bundle is now one
pom `artifactItem` + one capability line in its bnd, zero Java. The staging `artifactItem` and the
shade `<exclude>` remain the two pom faces of one declaration (the shade runs before the boot, so it
cannot be scanned), but that is the irreducible pom side — the Java hand-list is eliminated. This is the
same gesture as the registry above: *read the real artifact* (the capability the jar actually declares),
never a parallel hand-maintained list — `model-substrate-alignment` applied to the build. See
link:../osgi/osgi-boot-single-source-handoff.adoc[the OSGi boot single-source handoff].
====

[[the-boot-face]]
== The boot face — how each role loads (the seam, and why we keep losing it)

Ownership tells the roles apart at *design* time. But they also differ at *boot* time — in which
classloader each role's classes live — and that difference is the one we keep forgetting and then
rediscovering the hard way (a split class, a silent `ClassCastException` across the host seam). This
section pins it down so it stays pinned.

The host (`seed-master` and the two CLIs) runs *flat, outside OSGi* on the JVM application classloader
(JCL). It boots an embedded Felix and reads services back out of it — e.g.
`osgiRuntime.awaitService(ManifestUpdateGate.class)`, a `-port` type. For that typed handoff to work
(no reflection, no `ClassCastException`), the flat host *and* the installed bundle must share *one copy*
of the `-port` class. There is exactly one mechanism for one shared copy: the *system bundle* exports
the package (`FRAMEWORK_SYSTEMPACKAGES_EXTRA`), so every bundle imports the host's class rather than
loading its own. That shared-export boundary is the *seam*.

[IMPORTANT]
====
*The seam law (the load-bearing invariant).* A class of a `-port` is loaded by the *host JCL* and shared
into the framework via a system export. A class of a *domain* (`-core`) or an *edge* is loaded by its
*bundle classloader* and never escapes to the JCL. Equivalently, stated as the guard:

* `type=model` / `type=edge` package ⟹ exported by *its bundle only*, **never** in
  `system.packages.extra`. Leaking it there gives two copies (flat + bundle) and splits the class.
* `type=seam` package (and genuinely non-OSGi third-party jars with no `Bundle-SymbolicName`) ⟹
  exported by the *system bundle*, shared with the flat host. This is deliberate, and the *only* thing
  that legitimately belongs there.

A `-port` carries a `Bundle-SymbolicName` (it is a real jar), so "has a BSN ⟹ install it as a bundle"
is *too strong* — it would install the `-port` as its own bundle, give the host a second copy, and break
the very typed handoff the seam exists for. The discriminator is not "is it a bundle" but "which side of
the seam does it load on", and that is *declared*, not inferred.
====

So the embed namespace `io.nxmatic.rke2lab.embed` carries a fourth `type`, the seam, alongside the three
ownership roles' boot faces:

[cols="1,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| `type=` | Role | Boot face

| `model`
| domain (`-core`) with `@Component`
| Installed as a bundle (BundleCL). Its exported packages stay its own — never system-exported.

| `edge`
| `<target>-edge` with `@Component`
| Installed as a bundle (BundleCL). Same: its packages never leak to the system bundle.

| `seam`
| `-port` (the membrane)
| NOT installed as a bundle. System-exported so the flat host shares one typed copy. The JCL side of
  the boundary.

| `fixture`
| test-only carrier (+ `suite`/`role`)
| Installed only by the test harness (`FelixFrameworkExtension`), never on a runtime classpath.
|===

The one question the `type=seam` value answers — the key phrase to keep:

[quote]
____
*Which side of the seam frontier does the port load on?* — the system bundle (JCL), shared with the flat
host, or its own bundle (BundleCL), kept inside the framework.
____

That is the whole meaning of the value, and it is *factual, not metaphorical*: in OSGi exactly one
classloader may *define* a given package; everyone else *imports* that one copy (one package = one
exporter = one class). So "which side does it load on" is just "which classloader defines this package —
the system bundle, or a specific bundle?". `type=seam` declares the answer is *the system bundle* (so the
flat host and the bundles share one typed copy); `type=model`/`type=edge` declares the answer is *that
bundle alone* (and the system bundle must never also define it, or the two copies split the class).

Why a *declared* `type=seam` and not an inferred "no `@Component` ⟹ seam": inference is negative and
fragile — the day a `-port` grows a component it would silently flip from seam to domain and the seam
would break with no warning. Declaring the seam positively, in the same namespace as the rest, is the
single-source-of-truth gesture applied here: the bundle *says* which side of the frontier it loads on,
the boot reads it, nothing guesses. The guard then fail-fasts (naming the package) if a
`type=model`/`type=edge` package would ever reach `system.packages.extra`.

The boot machinery that reads this seam law — the pure `BootPlanner` that derives `system.packages.extra`
and runs the guard, told apart from the `FrameworkLauncher` that enacts it — is decomposed in
link:../osgi/osgi-boot-decomposition-spec.adoc[the OSGi boot decomposition]. Its pax/JCL volet sharpens
the same frontier from the build side: a bundle (BundleCL) that leaks onto the flat host classpath (JCL)
is a *wrong classpath*, so the boot stack is `optional` and staged by coordinate rather than left flat.

== The decisive test — domain vs edge

[quote]
____
Does this type *cross a boundary of the system*, or stay inside it reasoning over the model?
____

* Crosses → *edge*.
* Stays inside, computes → *domain* (or *port*, if it is the contract the domain calls).

The trap the playability axis dispels: the discriminator is *not* "does I/O / spawns a process". A
domain may read a file; `ProcessBuilder` and `java.nio` are playable. The discriminator is *reaching an
external target* — the skin of the system, not the act of I/O.

== The ownership rule (the load-bearing claim)

*A port belongs to the consumer that calls outward — the domain or the orchestration. The edge
IMPLEMENTS that port and PROVIDES the capability behind it; it never owns the port.* This is
hexagonal/DIP stated for our layout: the adapter depends on the contract, the contract never depends on
the adapter.

Consequences, both observed on the real reactor:

* There is *no `pulumi-port`*. The ports `pulumi-edge` implements — `SnapshotSource`,
  `InterventionLedgerWriter` — live in `doctor-port`, because *doctor* is the consumer that needs to read
  state and write a ledger. Nobody ever created a port named after the edge.
* A pure point of contact with *no domain* (ssh-to-age: it reasons over nothing, it just converts by
  shelling a tool) gets *no `-core` and no port of its own* — only an `-edge`, plus a port owned by
  whoever calls it.

The arrow that matters: `edge ┄implements┄▶ port ◀──calls── domain`. Both point AT the port; the port
points at nothing. That is why it is pure, and why it is the one door reached identically from either
world.

== Direction is a method, not a module, not two ports

A second recurring confusion: expecting an edge to split into an "in" port and an "out" port.

* *One edge = one target = one door.* It is not directional. The same `pulumi-edge` both reads (ingress)
  and writes (egress) — splitting it by direction would lie about it being one tool, and `ingress`
  collides with the k8s sense anyway.
* *Two ports does not mean in/out.* `pulumi-edge` implements two ports because the *domain* (doctor) has
  two distinct needs — read a snapshot, write a ledger — so they are named *by concern*
  (`SnapshotSource`, `InterventionLedgerWriter`), never `…In`/`…Out`.
* *Direction lives in the method name / prose.* `SnapshotSource.timeline()` is ingress;
  `LedgerWriter.up()` is egress. If a single contract one day both reads and writes, the edge stays one
  module with methods of both directions.

== Worked example B — ssh-to-age-edge (a point of contact, NO domain)

ssh-to-age reasons over nothing: it converts a key by shelling an external tool. There is no residual
computation if you remove the tool — so *no `-core`, and no port named after it*. The contract belongs to
the *consumer* that needs the age key.

Per the prerequisite design, the synthesis unit must not *fetch* the key mid-synthesis. The consumer is
`DefaultManifestSynthesisService` — the `@Component` (OSGi world, our lock): it runs a *pre-synthesis
pass* that calls the converter and *binds the age key into the profile-bearing context*, the same channel
that already carries `IncusIdentityMaterial` / `BootstrapIdentity`. That channel is
`ManifestSynthesisContext` (the request/ThreadLocal-scoped profile holder) — NOT `ManifestsUnitContext`,
which is the per-unit record (`chart`, `domainId`, `resolver`) and carries no profile. `doSynthesize`
then only reads the key from the context and builds the Secret:

Two modules for the contact (`ssh-to-age-edge` + the consumer's port), *zero* `-core`, *zero*
`ssh-to-age-port`. And the unit goes pure: synthesis *takes* the key as a prerequisite, it does not go
*fetch* it. (This edge is also the first one materialised in the OSGi world — ssh-to-age is playable, so
the edge need not be pushed to host; materialising it is about making the system boundary explicit, not
about playability.)

== The two principles this settles

. *Synthesis takes prerequisites; it does not fetch them.* A describe-step is a pure function of its
  inputs. Reaching out for an input mid-describe is the defect — independent of which world does the
  reaching.
. *The edge materialises the boundary even when the domain could do the contact inline.* An edge is not
  a playability concession; it is the system's skin made explicit. Playability decides only *which world*
  the edge lives in, never *whether* it is one.

== Quick decision guide

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
| You have… | Then…

| Logic that reasons over the model, no external contact
| `-core` (domain). Owns the ports it calls.

| The contract a consumer calls outward
| `-port`, owned by that consumer. Pure. Named by concern, never `…In/…Out`.

| An adapter that contacts one external target
| `<target>-edge`. Implements a consumer's port. Owns NO port, has NO `-core` unless it also carries real
  domain logic (it usually doesn't).

| A pure point of contact with no reasoning (ssh-to-age)
| Just the `<target>-edge` + a port owned by its consumer. No `-core`, no port of its own.
|===
