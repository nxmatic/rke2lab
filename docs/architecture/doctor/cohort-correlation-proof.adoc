= Cross-patient cohort correlation — the proof record
:toc: left
:toclevels: 3

[NOTE]
====
*Status:* PROOF RECORD — the empirical evidence behind the shipped HealthSystem keystone. This began
as a throwaway-or-promote spike (`spike/doctor-cohort-correlation`) answering one question before the
keystone committed to cross-patient correlation; the mechanism held, so it was promoted into
`feature/healthsystem-keystone` and shipped. This document is kept as the record of *what was proven
and how* — the design (link:healthsystem-access-control-design.adoc[]) cites it as the reason the
read half of cross-patient correlation was known-good before the gate was built. The vocabulary here
is the shipped one (`ClinicianId`/`Patient`/`grant`), not the spike-era jargon.
====

== The question it answered

Can the doctor, during a `pulumi preview`, *reach a second patient's medical record, fold across the
cohort, and surface a cross-patient finding*? Cross-patient correlation was expected to be
load-bearing for the future system, so this was proven — early and cheaply — before the keystone
committed to it.

== What it reused (not built)

The read substrate was already done; the proof added one primitive on top:

* `LiveMedicalRecordRegistry.recordFor(Patient)` is *backend-bound, not stack-bound*
  (`StackHandle.forBackend(backendDir, patient.project(), patient.stack())`) — it already
  reconstructs ANY patient's record in the same backend. No registry change to read a 2nd patient.
* `StackHistoryFixture.at(backendDir, project, stack)` seeds a real-shape history (a failed
  `Observation` + a `Prescription`, `seeded` tag) for any named stack — the "fake failure +
  diagnosis + prescription" for a 2nd patient, in the doctor's test layer (not by mutating dev).
* `policy.preview().simulate(scenarioId)` injects a fake symptom *during preview only* (never a real
  `up`) — the "simulate the same failure in preview" hook, already wired into `SystemdAdapterStage`.
* `MedicalRecord.efficacyOf(Symptom)` already computes whether a treatment held (no recurrence at the
  next visit) — reused per-patient, then folded across patients.

== The one new primitive — cohort enumeration

There was NO stack enumeration: the registry read a patient only if handed its identity; nothing
discovered "what other patients exist in this backend." So the proof added:

* `PulumiBackendLayout.stacksDir(backendDir, project)` → `<backendDir>/.pulumi/history/<project>` —
  the directory whose children are the stacks (patients).
* `MedicalRecordRegistry.cohortFor(Patient current)` → enumerate the sibling stacks under
  `stacksDir`, map each to a `Patient`, `recordFor` each, return the cohort. In the shipped code this
  is *ungated at the registry level by design* — the `(ClinicianId, Patient)` grant filter is applied
  one layer up by `ClinicalAccess`.

== Phase 1 — test-harness proof (fast, isolated, deterministic)

A JUnit test in `controlplane.bdd` (`LiveMedicalRecordRegistryCohortTest`, still in the suite):

* `StackHistoryFixture` seeds TWO patients in a `@TempDir` backend:
** `dev` — hits `connection-refused` (the patient in the room);
** `peer` — a prior `connection-refused` visit prescribed `RESTART_UNIT`, then a LATER visit with no
   recurrence (so `efficacyOf` shows it *resolved*).
* Run `cohortFor(devPatient)` → fold across both records.
* ASSERT the finding: connection-refused seen on 2 patients; ≥1 prior `RESTART_UNIT` attempt resolved
  it (the fold reads `peer`'s `efficacyOf(CONNECTION_REFUSED)`).

== Phase 2 — thin live-preview confirmation

* Seed a sibling stack in the real `.pulumi-state` backend (fixture), give it the resolved
  `connection-refused` history.
* `pulumi preview` on dev with `policy.preview.simulate` injecting `connection-refused`; watch the
  cross-patient finding appear in the preview output (the `⚕`-style readiness log).
* Confirms the mechanism IN SITU — real engine, real backend, lock-free read under preview.

== Findings (2026-06-10, both phases run)

*Verdict: the architecture handles cross-patient correlation. PROMOTABLE — and since promoted.*

*Phase 1 (isolated unit, `LiveMedicalRecordRegistryCohortTest`):* enumerate-and-fold worked first
try. Two patients seeded in a temp backend; `cohortFor(dev)` returned both records (current first),
and the fold read `peer`'s `efficacyOf(connection-refused).everWorked() == true` while `dev`'s own was
`false` — the benefit comes *only* from the sibling. No surprise in enumeration or `Patient` mapping;
globbing `stacksDir` → `Patient(org, project, <dir>)` is all it took.

*Phase 2 (in situ, real `pulumi preview` with `simulate: connection-refused` on `dev`):* the doctor
logged, from a live preview:
....
⚕ consulted with 283 prior visit(s); connection-refused seen 0× before
⚕ cohort: connection-refused seen on 1 of 2 patient(s); 1 prior treatment(s) resolved it
....
The per-patient view alone was BLIND (`dev` has 283 visits but 0 prior `connection-refused` — it
predates the doctor); the cohort view delivered the benefit (the `peer` sibling's resolved treatment).
Confirmed lock-free under preview (the self-read proof held), degraded-mode provisioning continued
unaffected, and the seeded `peer` stack + `simulate` config were torn down after — no trace left in
the real backend.

*Key structural learning:* the registry needed NO change to its read core; `cohortFor` is a thin
enumerate-then-`recordFor`-fold, and the pure clinical methods (`historyOf`/`efficacyOf`) fold across
patients UNCHANGED. That the per-patient domain model composes into a population view for free is the
strongest signal the model was shaped right — and the cheapest possible validation of the keystone
before committing to it.

== What the keystone added on top (now shipped)

The proof established the READ half, ungated. The shipped HealthSystem keystone added, per the design:
(1) the `(ClinicianId, Patient)` *grant filter* over the cohort, applied by `ClinicalAccess`; (2) the
keystone wiring that mints the grants at admission; (3) the single construction site replacing the
ad-hoc doctor wiring. The one *not-yet-built* piece remains referral-derived grants (the cohort grant
is minted from an interim same-backend rule today) — see the design's "grant seam awaits referrals".
