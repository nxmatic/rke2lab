== The model — confirmed from the design, corrected against my own conflation

Two facts the reading settled, both load-bearing and both places I had it wrong first:

. *This is the realisation of the CELLAR role — one of the five gardening roles, defined at
  link:gardening-model-spec.adoc[the gardening model].* The model states, and this doc does NOT re-derive:
  the cellar is the COMMISSIONER's conservation organ (not a third actor, not the gardener's); one
  gardening = one cellar; the végétal is opaque, addressed by a neutral `Parcel`, never read. What THIS
  realisation adds — the *how* over the Pulumi backend:
  ** the three verbs are pure Pulumi-backend gestures: `store`/`fetch` = write/read a `végétal` as a stack
     OUTPUT keyed by coordinate slug (`createOrSelectStack(project, stack)`), `neighbours` = enumerate the
     sibling stacks under the same soil. `Parcel(project, stack)` IS a Pulumi stack reference said in
     gardening words.
  ** the INTERFACE (`Cellar`, `Parcel`) is homed in `seed-broker-port` (the neutral fondation seam)
     because its face must speak gardening; the IMPL is a host object in `pulumi-edge`, laid into Felix at
     boot by `registerService` (§ open question #4, resolved) — because THIS system realises the gardener
     as a DS singleton in one Felix, so one gardening = one framework = one laid cellar.
  ** the doctor merely PROJECTS onto it (`Patient ↔ Parcel`, `Report ↔ végétal`); the three fusing classes
     carried doctor names, but that WAS the leak the de-seam exposes — all three do the one thing: persist
     onto the Pulumi state. The doctor is the cellar's first consumer, not its owner.

. *`store` of a COMPOSITE asks the gardener "what is this?"; `store` of a single part is verbatim
  (corrected 2026-07-12 — the model's link:gardening-model-spec.adoc#cellar-asks[cellar-asks] law).* The
  cellar does not open the sachet and does not let the caller say where. Recognising two cases:
  ** *ledger (intervention)* — a single sealed part: filed verbatim under its coordinate, no split. This is
     the degenerate case (nothing to decompose), and it is ALREADY opaque in the shipped
     `PulumiInterventionLedgerWriter`/`StackInterventionJournal` — only the leak NAMES remain
     (`OUTPUT_KEY="interventions"`, `DoctorCoordinate.INTERVENTION`).
  ** *medical-record (visit)* — a COMPOSITE: the cellar (holding the opaque envelope) asks the gardener
     `sow(SplitCoordinate(domain), envelope)`, gets back neutral parts (`{ rootstock → { role → value } }`
     — drawers and cases, `Role.FRUIT`/`Role.SOWING`), and files each part verbatim in its case. The split
     is the STORE mechanism for a composite, not a separate later concern — the shipped `DoctorSplitReflector`
     exists precisely to answer this.

*The real leak the cartography exposed (2026-07-12).* Splitting a visit by case is CORRECT and neutral
(`Role.*` is furniture vocabulary, not a doctor word — "one does not mix the seeds"). The leak is that
today `StackMedicalRecordJournal.visitEnvelope` RECOMPOSES a `VisitWire` (a doctor type) host-side from the
two role-keyed outputs, and the OSGi reader then `codec.decode(…, VisitWire.class)`. The fix is directional,
not flattening: the host `fetch` returns the case contents OPAQUE (sealed sachets, addressed by case), and
the `{cases} → Visit` recomposition moves to the DOMAIN (`MedicalRecordReader`, OSGi). Today it is
inverted (host recomposes, OSGi decodes); the furniture model says the host files/returns neutral cases and
the doctor assembles.

The one mechanical change that makes CRUD opaque: `StackSnapshot.outputsNamed` stops being keyed on a
domain FIELD name (`"consultationReport"`, `OUTPUT_KEY="interventions"`) and starts being keyed on the
neutral case/coordinate (`Role.*` / the envelope shell), never a doctor field. That single reshape kills
both leak names.

[[every-scion-contributes]]
== Every scion contributes — the cellar IS the resource producer (the `ResourceManager` dissolution)

The doctor is the cellar's FIRST consumer, not its only one. The general law: *every scion contributes its
harvest to the cellar, and — on the Pulumi realisation — `store` PRODUCES the Pulumi resource.* This is where
the host's old `ResourceManager` dissolves.

*The SCION harvests AND stores — it does not return its harvest to the host.* Since the host lays the `Cellar`
into Felix at boot (`registerService(Cellar.class)`), a scion resolves it in-container via
`ScenarioRegistry.require(Cellar.class)`, the same way it resolves its contact and the `RunGate`. When it
harvests, it `store`s — the grower reaps AND puts away (the lexicon law: *the grower draws on the cellar, the
gardener merely ROUTES*). There is NO harvest field on the `RunbookEnvelope` and no host re-store: routing a
harvest back out only to call `store` from the host would be an absurd round-trip. The envelope keeps carrying
`(runbook, consultations)` — the doctor's consultations cross because the host GRAFTS them into the runbook
(narration); the harvest does not cross because its destination is the cellar, not the runbook (two
destinations, two channels — do not conflate them, the mistake the doctor's consultation channel invites).

*What the scion needs to store.* Two ambient facts of the run, both resolved from the registry: the `Cellar`
(the neutral furniture) and the `Parcel` currently being cultivated. The `Parcel` is published into Felix as an
AMBIENT fact — the twin of `RunGate` — because the run cultivates ONE parcel (the master stack) and only the
host knows its identity at the GIVEN. The `Cellar` stays NEUTRAL and addressed FROM OUTSIDE (`store(Parcel,
…)`): it must not hold "the current parcel" as state — the doctor addresses N parcels (one per patient), so a
mono-parcel or sentinel-resolving cellar would break it. The current parcel lives BESIDE the cellar (an ambient
fact), never inside it.

*The dissolution.* On the former host bootstrap, a `ResourceManager` ran `ResourceCreationPipeline` and, per
scion, built a "thin graph mirror of the result + a `dependsOn` edge" (`SystemdAdapterResource`,
`SeedImageBuildResource`, `SeedManifestSynthResource`, `ClusterReadinessResource`). That is EXACTLY what
`PulumiCellar.store` already does: an out-of-run `up()` registers a `CellarEntry` (`ComponentResource`) whose
payload rides `registerOutputs` under the coordinate slug. So there is no `ResourceManager` to re-create in the
new system — producing a resource per scion IS the scion's `Cellar.store`; the Pulumi realisation turns each
stored case into a resource.

=== Two Pulumi natures: RESOURCES (the graph) vs OUTPUTS (top-level exports)

* *Resources* — the graph nodes carrying identity + the `dependsOn` edge. Produced by `store`.
* *Outputs* — the top-level `ctx.export(name, value)` the commissioner reads. They are a PROJECTION of the
  resources (the former `OutputBuilder` merely republished the resources' URNs/summaries flat). The cellar
  already files as outputs-OF-a-resource, *bypassing* top-level `ctx.export` — the top-level is a READ FAÇADE,
  never the append-only store.

*The read façade stays HOST, carries no métier.* `ctx.export` is the twin of `Cellar.fetch`: the commissioner
re-reads the façade of the stored cases. The façade is host (like the gRPC mount — the commissioner reads it),
and holds NO decision — it lifts, it does not decide.

=== The cellar consults the RunGate — two spaces: CONSERVED (`up`) vs PRE-RESERVE (`preview`)

A harvest must not be LOST in dry-run — but preview must touch nothing conserved. The cellar reconciles this
because it is a system tool: like every tool it consults the ambient `RunGate` and ADAPTS, so the scion holds
ONE verb (`store(Parcel, harvest)`) and never chooses the mode.

[cols="1,1,1",options="header"]
|===
| gate | Pulumi gesture | space
| open (cultivating) | `stack.up()` | the CONSERVED state — the real resources, the history fold
| closed (surveying) | `stack.preview()` | the PRE-RESERVE — the plan of what WOULD be produced this run
|===

This is NOT "fabricate a fake harvest" (the lexicon's red line): `preview()` mutates nothing and invents no
green — it is the REAL tool computing the plan honestly, which IS the act of surveying (lexicon: *the gardener
plans without cultivating, nothing is touched*). So a dry-run does not lose the harvest: it lands in the
pre-reserve (the plan), rendered PENDING in the runbook via E9; a live run folds it into the conserved state.

*The pre-reserve is EPHEMERAL — the plan of THIS run, never an inter-run history.* A preview plan conserved and
re-read next run would be exactly the cache-that-can-lie the model forbids; `pulumi preview` is ephemeral by
nature (no `--save-plan`), so this holds. The RunGate decision lives IN the cellar (the `PulumiCellar` reads the
ambient gate and routes `up`/`preview`) — the twin of every other tool consulting the gate, not a new
orchestrator.

*Why the runbook alone does not carry the harvest (the trap avoided).* jGiven's `ReportModel` captures STEP
ARGUMENTS (the `@Quoted` values), NOT the `@ProvidedScenarioState`/`@ExpectedScenarioState` — so a scion's
harvest held in scenario state is NOT recoverable from the runbook. The runbook carries the narration/plan
(steps, PENDING, quoted counts), the cellar carries the data (conserved or pre-reserved). Two channels, two
destinations — never conflate "the plan is in the runbook" with "the data is in the runbook".

=== A harvest is FETCHED from the cellar, never pushed as an input (amont ≠ récolte)

Now that harvests are conserved at the cellar, a scion that needs ANOTHER scion's harvest FETCHES it from the
cellar — it is never pushed into an input/amendment. The SOIL (and any amendment) carries only the *where* / the
*policy facet* — a plot path, a boolean posture — NEVER a cultivated value (a fingerprint, a urn, a checksum).
Those are harvests; they live at the cellar and are re-read by address.

The trap this forbids (a vestige of the former host bootstrap): `ManifestSynthesisRequest` still has an
`imageState` field (carrying `imageFingerprint`, a Stage-A image harvest). In the scion world it is NOT wired
into `ManifestsRunbookInput` — the synthesis scion defaults it to `ImageState.unknown()`. It must STAY unwired:
when the synthesis genuinely needs the fingerprint, the manifests scion FETCHES the image scion's harvest from
the cellar (`Cellar.fetch`), it does not receive it amended. So incus, consulting manifests, forwards the SOIL
(the plot) — never the fingerprint. The ordering that makes this sound is the causal `dependsOn` below: the
image is cultivated and stored BEFORE the synthesis that fetches it.

=== The `dependsOn` edge is CAUSAL — the amendment graph, not the runbook position

The persisted Pulumi DAG must mirror real causality, not narration order. The edge is read from the AMENDMENT
graph (amont) AND the fetch graph: if scion incus FETCHED the image scion's harvest from the cellar (or was
amended from provisioning state that depends on it), then `incus.resource dependsOn image.resource` falls out of
that consumption. NOT from the scion declaring "I depend on systemd" (that would re-couple the scion to sibling
soils — forbidden), and NOT merely from the step's position in the runbook tree (that reads a POSITION, not a
CAUSE). The runbook nesting and the causal graph converge, but the causal graph (what a scion fetched / was
amended with) is the correct source.

=== The scion — not the façade — decides what has output value

Who knows a harvest field is worth surfacing? The SCION, never the host façade (a façade that "picks the
fingerprint" would re-take métier knowledge host-side — forbidden). The scion marks its harvest by the neutral
role it already uses: `@Scion(Role.FRUIT)` = conservable value, `@Scion(Role.SOWING)` = internal expectations.
The split files each part in its case; the host façade then MECHANICALLY lifts whatever is marked `FRUIT`,
knowing nothing of its meaning (it never names `seedImageFingerprint`). Sovereignty: *the scion decides the
value, the host executes the projection.*

*Granularity (current vs planned).* Today the marked grain is the WHOLE FRUIT part — the fruit case becomes one
output (`stack output <domain>.fruit` → the JSON blob), reusing the existing role, inventing no split
vocabulary. A FINER grain — the scion marking WHICH fields are flat top-level scalars (recovering
`seedImageFingerprint = "abc123"` as a named string) — enriches the split vocabulary (a `flat` facet or a
dedicated role) and is a PLANNED EVOLUTION, expected to land during the top-down migration when a concrete
`stack output X` flat-scalar need appears. Not deferred-and-forgotten: the migration WILL hit it.

[[host-tree-provisioning]]
== The host tree the instance mounts — publish / validate / mount (2026-07-14)

The scions conserve harvests at the cellar (above). This section is the twin question on the OTHER
matter a run produces: the FILESYSTEM TREE the incus instance mounts (the former `host/` — its
`rke2-manifests.d` / `k8s-daemonset.d` / `systemd.d`, the cloud-config, the env-overlay). It is a
DISTINCT concern from a conserved harvest — a harvest is a value in the cellar; this is bytes on the
host filesystem the instance binds. The former host (`main`) solved it with an FS slot-rotation
(`docs/architecture/systemd/host-slot-management.adoc` — replica slots → `rsync -av --delete` into
the live `host/`). That FS mechanism is KEPT; what the scion/cellar idiom adds is WHERE the contract
and validation live (below), not a replacement of the rsync.

=== The invariants (the result, non-negotiable)

* *R1 — never lose what a live instance mounts.* A live instance binds physical host paths (the mount
  DIRECTORIES `rke2-manifests.d` / `k8s-daemonset.d` / `systemd.d` under `host/`). Those DIRECTORIES
  are never destroyed — only their CONTENT is synced. `rsync -av --delete` from the chosen replica into
  the live `host/` is exactly the tool: it lazily creates missing dirs and syncs file content, but a
  mount directory present on BOTH sides (it is in every replica) is never deleted. So the bind never
  points at a vanished inode.
* *R2 — a cache of the produced host trees,* to compare and validate evolutions/corrections.
* *R3 — a host tree IS the root that materialises the instance's assets* (and MAY later carry the
  run's narration: the runbook rendered `adoc` for the operator, `json` for the runtime).
* *R4 — at LIVE provisioning, reconcile the live `host/` with the replica produced this run* (rsync);
  at PREVIEW, produce the replica but do NOT touch `host/`.

=== The model — the three fixed places + the CONTRACT in the cellar

Three FIXED filesystem places, a pipeline, not variable modes:

  host.staging.N  →  host.live  →  host.gc.N
    (produced)        (deployed)     (evicted delta)

* *`host.staging.N`* — where a run MATERIALISES its replica (the synthesis writes here). A bounded
  rotation over THREE increments (0/1/2); each `host.staging.N` is IMMUTABLE once written (the cache,
  R2). The rotation recycles positions — a new run overwrites the oldest position with a fresh replica.
* *`host.live`* — the PHYSICAL directory (not a symlink), the STABLE bind-mount target of the instance.
  Singular (one mounted tree at a time). It is a FAITHFUL replica of the `host.staging.N` it was
  `rsync -av --delete`d from — nothing writes into `host.live` except that rsync from a staging, so
  every byte traces to an identified staging (immutable-as-mirror: a provenance guarantee, not a frozen
  FS). At LIVE the chosen staging is rsynced in (content synced, mount dirs preserved — R1); at PREVIEW
  `host.live` is NOT touched (the RunGate closes the rsync, the twin of how it closes the cellar `up()`).
* *`host.gc.N`* — the `--backup-dir` of the rsync: what a sync EVICTS from `host.live` (files
  overwritten/deleted as live moves to its new staging) — the negative delta, bounded rotation of 3.

*ONE manifest for the whole collection, living at the cellar.* There is a SINGLE host-manifest — the
index of the replica collection, one entry per replica present on the FS (`staging.0/1/2`, `gc.0/1/2`,
`live`), and it is CONSERVED AT THE CELLAR (a `Cellar.store`), not a file the host keeps on the side.
Two natures of entry, both referencing a `host.staging.{SN}` (one kind of referent everywhere — never
an invented live-identity):

  host.staging.{SN}  → its CONTRACT: per-tree checksums + provenance   (IMMUTABLE once written)
  host.live          → host.staging.{SN} it currently mirrors          (the ONLY mutable entry)
  host.gc.{GN}       → host.staging.{SN} it evicted from live           (IMMUTABLE until GN recycled)

The collection is almost entirely IMMUTABLE — the staging and gc entries never change once written
(until their slot is recycled by the bounded rotation). The SOLE moving part is the `host.live` entry:
it is overwritten `→ host.staging.{SN}` on each live provisioning. PREVIEW-vs-PROMOTED is DEDUCED from
it (a staging is preview-only while `host.live` does not name it; promoted once it does — no flag, the
relation is the reality). A `host.gc.{GN}` records the staging it EVICTED from live and stays frozen on
it until the GN rotation recycles the slot; so over successive provisionings `host.live` moves and the
gc entries stay fixed.

*Because the manifest lives at the cellar, the history of live promotions is FREE.* The cellar is
append-only, versioned by its stack history (`fetch` walks the timeline). Each mutation of the
`host.live` entry is a new `Cellar.store` → a new history version → the timeline of "which staging was
live when" falls out of the cellar, no separate transition log. R2's sliding cache IS that timeline.

*And because each staging entry carries CHECKSUMS, the cellar describes the FS in full — without ever
holding a byte of it.* The manifest is the complete, authoritative description of what each replica
must contain; the FS is merely a VERIFIABLE materialisation of it. This is the strong reading at its
limit: the cellar is the AUTHORITY over the host FS state while remaining pure storage of FACETS
(checksums + paths, never files — fetch-not-push, the twin of `IncusHarvest.soil`). What it buys, all
free: validation (§ below) is FS-vs-manifest-checksums; drift is detectable (a supposedly-immutable
staging whose bytes no longer match its checksums has been tampered with); comparing evolutions (R2) is
a checksum diff between two manifest versions in the history, never a re-read of the FS; and a lost
replica can be re-materialised from its description + the deterministic SOIL synthesis.

R2: the cache is the collection of `host.staging.N` (the last 3 produced) PLUS the `host.gc.N` (the
last 3 eviction deltas — the evolution history), INDEXED by the cellar's host-manifests. Comparing two
versions is a diff of conserved CHECKSUMS, not a re-read of two arbres; the no-op check (was main's
byte-for-byte replica-vs-`host/`) becomes a checksum diff.

*Who reads and writes it — two reads, two writers, all on the neutral 3-verb cellar.* The manifest is
NOT one document mutated in place: it is ENTRIES PER REPLICA, each an append-only `Cellar.store`; the
logical manifest is the FOLD of the parcel's timeline at read. Two writers, never in conflict (append):

* each *SCION* PUBLISHES its own staging entry — `store(parcel, {host.staging.{SN}, checksums,
  provenance})` — it knows only its own morceau (an availability).
* the *HOST at grow* PROMOTES — and ONLY it, because promoting is `rsync staging → host.live`: once the
  rsync SUCCEEDS, it appends ONE promotion envelope carrying both linked mutations `{host.live → SN,
  host.gc.{GN} → SN_evicted}` (the switch is a single atomic fact). Stored only post-rsync-success, so
  the manifest never lies about the FS.

Two reads, both derived from `fetch` (the whole timeline) — no new port verb, the cellar stays neutral
at three verbs:

* the *HEAD* (current state: where `host.live` points, which stagings exist) — a FOLD of the timeline,
  last-wins per key. The host (to promote) and incus (to validate) read the head. It is a convenience
  fold on the reader side (the doctor already folds its timeline the same way), NOT a new contract.
* the *TIMELINE* (all versions) — `fetch` verbatim, for R2 (comparing evolutions, the live-promotion
  history — free from the append-only store, § above).

*What is new vs `main` (the FS mechanism is NOT).* The `rsync -av --delete` into a physical `host.live`,
the staging/gc rotation — all kept from `main`. What the scion/cellar idiom ADDS: the CONTRACT moves to
the cellar as host-manifests (not `main`'s slot ConfigMap), PUBLISHED by each scion, VALIDATED by the
incus prep before the rsync. The manner of syncing bytes is `main`'s; the ownership of the contract and
the validation is the new idiom.

*A slot's IDENTITY is its POSITION* (`host.staging.N`), NOT its content hash — so there is no "no-op for
free by the name"; the no-op check stays an explicit checksum diff. The content hash lives INSIDE the
host-manifest (a comparison discriminant, correspondence hash→slot possible) but is NOT a key: the same
hash may map to SEVERAL slots (identical content re-materialised across the rotation), so the hash
discriminates, the position identifies.

=== Publish (each scion) vs VALIDATE (incus prep) vs mount (host grow)

The three roles do not overlap:

* *Each scion PUBLISHES what it makes available* — it materialises its own content under the SOIL and
  stores its host-manifest (pointer + checksums) at the cellar. It publishes an availability; it
  validates nothing beyond its own morceau (it only knows its own). Concretely the manifests scion
  (the sole scion that materialises an FS tree — the instance mounts chiefly the synthesis tree)
  checksums its staging replica with `HostTreeChecksummer` (relpath → SHA-256) and stores a
  `HostManifest` (`@SeedContract("host-manifest")`: the staging root, the checksums, the provenance)
  under the current parcel.
* *The incus PREP VALIDATES the content made available to the instance.* incus is already the prep
  (it consults manifests, assembles what the instance mounts) — validating that content is the natural
  conclusion of preparing, and only incus sees the WHOLE of what the instance will mount, so only it
  can validate global coherence. It fetches the published host-manifests and confronts the replica slot
  against the published checksums. A mismatch (corruption, partial write, an interrupted run) is caught
  BEFORE the rsync into `host/`, not discovered inside the instance — it becomes a symptom on incus's
  already-wired doctor channel (the `IMAGE_BUILD_FAILED` twin — a `CONTENT_MISMATCH` kind), fork B.
+
NOTE: *the re-check is DEFERRED — it earns nothing WITHIN a run.* In the same run, manifests just
synthesised the tree AND checksummed it (the publish step above); re-verifying those same checksums
in incus's prep immediately after proves nothing — nothing can have drifted between synthesis and
validation in one run. The check EARNS its keep only ACROSS runs: reading a staging produced by an
EARLIER run, or reconciling `host.live`, where corruption/tampering could have happened since. So the
publish (the checksums at the cellar) SHIPS now; the incus-side re-check is built when a cross-run
read actually consumes it. This also keeps incus decoupled from manifests (no `manifests-core`/algo
dependency) until the need is real — the FS access + `HostManifest` decode are wired only then.
* *The host GROW reconciles then mounts already-validated content* (Shape C, `com.pulumi`): it fetches,
  `rsync -av --delete`s the validated slot into the physical `host/` (mount dirs preserved — R1), and
  binds `host/` into the instance. It does NOT re-validate — re-validating host-side would duplicate
  metier logic outside the OSGi world (forbidden ownership leak); it trusts the prep.

*Filesystem access is not an obstacle (settled):* the incus scion runs in the SAME place as the host,
over the same FS — reading/checksumming `host.N` from the scion is free. `BootstrapPaths` already
offers place-dependent VIEWS (host / node) and new views can be added, so resolving `host.N` is a view,
not new plumbing.

=== Live vs preview

There is NO separate preview directory: a preview run materialises `host.staging.N` (the SAME place a
live run does) and its host-manifest, but the gate does NOT fire the rsync into `host.live` — so
`live.syncedFrom` never comes to name this staging, and PREVIEW-vs-PROMOTED is exactly that deduced
distinction (§ the model). `host.live` is left untouched (the live instance is unaffected). This is the
same RunGate axis as the cellar's `up`/`preview` (§ *the cellar consults the RunGate*), realised on the
host tree instead of the cellar store — one axis, two realisations: the gate closes the rsync exactly
as it closes the cellar `up()`. A preview is thus TRACEABLE from the cellar (a produced staging its
host-manifest records, that live never adopted), with no mode-directory polluting the FS.

=== The runbook `json` has a RUNTIME role INSIDE the node (not only operator narration)

R3 says a replica slot MAY carry the run's narration; the `json` rendering is more than a consultable
artefact — it earns a runtime job in the node. Because a live instance is never restarted when the
mounted content changes (R1: we rsync fresh content into `host/`, we do not reboot), a content change
must be reconciled by REFRESHING the affected services in the node (a targeted reload/restart), not a
reboot. The runbook `json` is what tells WHICH: it carries the mapping *changed content → services
to refresh*. It pairs exactly with the content validation: the incus prep validates THAT the content
is conform; the runbook `json` says WHAT to do when it changed — the checksum diff (new `host.N` vs
the previous) names the changed files, and the runbook maps those to the services to reload. So the
`json` travels inside `host.N` for the node's own refresh logic, while the `adoc` is the operator's
read. (Concrete shape — the change→service map, who consumes it in the node — is downstream work.)

*Dogfooding — ONE model, two renderings.* Both artefacts ARE the run's jGiven `ReportModel`: the
`adoc` is `AsciiDocReportGenerator` over the model (the operator narration); the `json` is
`ScenarioJsonWriter(model)` — the model's raw serialisation, the SAME format the scions already cross
the realm boundary with today, round-tripped by `ScenarioJsonReader`. The `json` runbook is therefore
NOT a bespoke artefact — it is the `ReportModel` itself, so the node reads it with machinery we
already have. This is the dogfooding: the jGiven model serves as narration AND as runtime artifact.

*The hard consequence.* A `ReportModel` captures only the `@Quoted` STEP ARGUMENTS (+ step names and
statuses), NOT `@ProvidedScenarioState`/`@ExpectedScenarioState` (§ the two channels — the runbook is
narration, the cellar is data). So for the node to read WHAT to refresh out of the `json`, that fact
must be NARRATED — exposed as a `@Quoted` step argument, not held in scenario state. A scion touching
content must SAY so in its step (e.g. "… refreshing `foo.service`"), so the affected service survives
into the serialised model. Healthy pressure: it forces the narration to be complete rather than
letting load-bearing facts hide in unrendered state.

== Open questions (2 of 4 resolved 2026-07-12; #2 + #3 remain)

. *`Parcel` arity — RESOLVED (2026-07-12): keep `Parcel(project, stack)` as shipped.* Since the Cellar
  is the Pulumi-backend frontier (§ <<the-model>> claim #1), `Parcel` IS a Pulumi stack reference; the
  `org` in the figure text is the Pulumi org — a host/doctor-side constant applied when projecting, not
  a field the neutral seam forces the host to invent. The figure text was loose; the shipped
  `(project, stack)` record stands.

. *What coordinate does `fetch` stamp? — RESOLVED (2026-07-12): NONE. Collect-all-neutral, capability
  as a service.* The guiding principle (the user's): the cellar is the LEAST-capable realisation — the
  host knows nothing of the doctor. So we do not ask it to be clever. If the OSGi side can already
  RENDER a service (interpret, assemble), it OFFERS it; it is up to the cellar to CONSUME that service or
  not, per what it can do. This is REST/HATEOAS turned toward storage: the neutral store returns
  everything neutral, and the interpretation CAPABILITY is a service the domain offers, never a
  requirement on the store. Concretely: `fetch(parcel)` returns `{version, when, + every present output
  by its NEUTRAL name}` (the `Role.*` cases) — NO doctor coordinate stamped, no doctor type. The OSGi
  reader — which asked for THIS parcel — knows what to assemble. The "which coordinate / which assembly"
  lives entirely on the capable side.
+
*The neutral shelf is Pulumi's NATIVE addressing, not an invented label (realisation detail).* The
cellar need not mint any coordinate: the Pulumi checkpoint already carries each resource's native
identity — the `type` token (e.g. `rke2lab:controlplane:SystemdAdapter`), the `urn`, the logical name —
alongside its `outputs` (see `StackSnapshot`, iterating `deployment.resources[]`). That native identity
IS the neutral shelf: the cellar addresses by what the SOIL already gives it, no doctor word. A different
furniture (a git store) would have its own native discriminant (path, blob sha) — the principle is the
same, the medium differs. So `fetch` hands back resources addressed by their Pulumi-native meta + neutral
outputs; the capable OSGi side filters/assembles by what it asked for.

. *`store` for the medical-record parcel vs the ledger parcel.* Today only the ledger is written
  (`append`); the medical record is written elsewhere (`SystemdAdapterResource` nests
  `FIELD_CONSULTATION_REPORT`/`FIELD_EXPECTATIONS` — the leak). The Cellar's `store` must become the
  ONE write path, keyed by coordinate slug. Whether `SystemdAdapterResource`'s write folds into
  `Cellar.store` now or in the domain-migration step is a scope call for the plan.

. *How the host registers the Cellar into Felix — RESOLVED (2026-07-12): `registerService(Cellar.class,
  hostImpl)`, NOT via the broker.* Confirmed against `DefaultSeedBroker` + seed-broker-spec
  § `edge-reaches-osgi`. The host publishes the Cellar ACTIVELY into Felix, but the target is the bare
  service registry, not the `SeedBroker`. Two load-bearing facts:
  ** *The broker never sees the Cellar, and must not.* `DefaultSeedBroker` collects ONLY `SeedHandler`s
     (growers projecting a metier onto the neutral) by constructor `@Reference(MULTIPLE)`. The host is
     outside Felix, cannot be a DS component, so contributes no handler. The Cellar is not a grower — it
     is a COLLABORATOR a handler's dependency (`MedicalRecordRegistry`) `@References`, two hops below the
     broker: `SeedBroker → SeedHandler → MedicalRecordRegistry → @Reference Cellar`.
  ** *The reference is STATIC + mandatory, so boot ORDER is the whole difficulty.* The host holds the
     single global start-level cursor (`StartLevelLever`, levels per role from `BootPlanner`), registers
     the Cellar while consumer bundles sit ABOVE the cursor, THEN raises it — the reference binds
     directly, no `greedy`/`dynamic`/`OPTIONAL`. A doctor with no cellar is a wiring bug that must fail
     loud.
  +
  This is STEP 2 territory (boot wiring), not the impl itself — sequenced by the plan, but the mechanism
  is now settled.

== Implementation plan (the next work session starts here)

*Guiding rule:* replace the broken API with the design's, smallest step that recompiles, verified
green before the next. Build per repo convention: `flox activate -- ./mvnw -pl <module> -am clean
verify -Dmaven.build.cache.skipCache=true -DskipTests=false` (NEVER `install`; count `Tests run:`).

. *Settle the two remaining open questions above* (slug-as-output-name, store scope) — a short design
  confirmation, no code. Parcel arity + register-timing are already resolved (2026-07-12). Blocks
  nothing else once answered.

. *FIRST CODE STEP — move the visit ASSEMBLY host→OSGi (resequenced 2026-07-12).* The "OSGi is stable"
  premise does NOT hold for the visit read path: `StackMedicalRecordJournal` RECOMPOSES a `VisitWire`
  host-side (`new VisitWire(…, outputsNamed(Role.FRUIT), outputsNamed(Role.SOWING))`) — a doctor type the
  host can no longer even name (it is why `pulumi-edge` fails to compile). Before the host Cellar can
  emerge cleanly, `MedicalRecordReader` (OSGi, where `VisitWire` now lives) must ASSEMBLE the visit from
  the neutral cases the cellar returns (`fruit`/`sowing`), instead of receiving a host-built `VisitWire`.
  Doing this FIRST is the whole point of the ordering: if the host Cellar came first, we would be forced
  to either break compilation or re-incarnate the host-side recomposition — the exact bad pattern the
  furniture model forbids. The read reflector (HATEOAS) is NOT on this path (the doctor fetches by a KNOWN
  coordinate — the address suffices); it stays designed-not-coded, out of scope here.

. *Write the host `Cellar` impl* (working name `PulumiCellar` / `StackCellar` — pick in step 1) in
  `pulumi-edge`, implementing `store`/`fetch`/`neighbours` as the conservation ROLE over a composition of
  furnitures: `fetch` returns the neutral CASES opaque (no `VisitWire`, no doctor import); `store` files a
  single part verbatim (ledger) and asks the gardener to split a composite (visit) — the cellar is itself
  a broker CLIENT for the composite store. No `FIELD_*`, no `OUTPUT_KEY`.

. *Retire the three fused classes* (`StackMedicalRecordJournal`, `StackInterventionJournal`,
  `PulumiInterventionLedgerWriter`) and the leak constants (`InterventionLedgerLayout.OUTPUT_KEY`, any
  `FIELD_*`), updating their tests to target the one `Cellar` impl.

. *Compile `pulumi-edge` green.* This is the milestone: the doctor-frontier host impl exists, the dead
  doctor.port/records imports are gone.

. *Then re-run the seed-master compile* to surface the NEXT wall (bbox.port, FrameworkLaunchPipeline,
  the fluent `pipeline.*`) — the top-down march resumes from a compiling `pulumi-edge`.
