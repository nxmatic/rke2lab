The user named this as a standing ritual (2026-06-21), peer to the atlas's monotone-additivity proof.
Where additivity proves a new model breaks NOTHING (negative), this proves a new TERM earns its place
by giving new expressive power (positive/generative).

## The ritual — two artifacts, every time a term/verb enters the glossary

1. **Vocabulary view** — a figure placing the new term among its neighbours: which axis it belongs to,
   what it composes with, what it collides with. Read faithfully from the code/glossary, in the
   preview ([[diagram-preview-file]]), en-US, code-faithful identifiers. This is
   [[brainstorm-vocabulary-view-first]]'s "Diagram 0" applied to a single term, not a whole design.
2. **Before/after capability sentences** — "before we could NOT say X / after we CAN say X", each
   pinned to a REAL artifact in the system, colored by horizon (blue=sayable now / green=unlocked next
   / amber=design horizon). The term is VALIDATED only if the sentences are both new and true; a term
   that licenses no new sentence is dead weight (the same two-way coverage test as the capability map).

## Why it works (the user's point)

A term is not validated by its definition but by what it lets you SAY about the running system.
Rendering the sentences against real artifacts (e.g. "a unit takes body as a bundle in the live Felix
— load-from-store") forces the term to prove it carries weight, and surfaces collisions at naming time
(e.g. "manifestation" collided with `manifest`; replaced by "embodiment"). The horizon coloring makes
the term's sentences double as a roadmap fragment.

## Worked example (the one that birthed the rule)

Introducing the unit-repository resolution + substrate vocabulary: `universe` ⊃ `constellation` (the
closure that answers a need) ⊃ `star` (a unit) on the resolution axis; `embodiment` (a unit takes body
in a substrate — bundle JVM / workload cluster / checkpoint Pulumi / tree at rest) on the orthogonal
substrate axis. Validated by anchored sentences ("recruiting = the resolver traces a constellation,
each star takes body in its substrate; migration by a pull is a star changing embodiment"). The
celestial register was chosen to fit the existing glossary (see rke2lab
`system-space-world-universe-glossary`).
