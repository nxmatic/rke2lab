= Target module layout — the host/OSGi decomposition
:toc: left
:toclevels: 3

[NOTE]
====
*Status:* TARGET (the destination layout, not a slice). This spec freezes WHERE the host/OSGi
decomposition is heading — the three top-level spaces (`osgi/` / `host/` / `exec/`), the three orthogonal
axes (purity, direction, nature), and the module roster every class lands in. Sub-decomposition (which
exact class goes in which module) is OUT OF SCOPE here; that is per-slice cartography work. This document
is the compass; slices are the steps.

It lives in `wip/specs/` and graduates into the durable atlas (`docs/architecture/integration-atlas.adoc`,
the "two spaces" section it extends) when the layout starts materialising.

*Backup point.* `main` was tagged `backup/pre-decomposition-2026-06-17` (annotated, signed, pushed to
origin) at commit `66bed704` — the pre-decomposition state (Step 2 slices 1–2 shipped: BootstrapConfig
relocate + the OSGi bench). Return there to recover the monolithic layout.
====

== 1. Why this layout

Step 2 proved, on the real Felix engine (the OSGi bench), that this application IS a ports-and-adapters
system whose wiring is OSGi resolution: a bundle declares what it needs (`Require`) and offers
(`Provide`) in its manifest, and the resolver wires need to provider. The target layout makes that
structural. The integration atlas already names the deepest seam — the *two spaces*, OSGi (pure,
describes) vs host (actualises over Pulumi); this layout turns that seam into the top-level directory
split, adds the second axis (direction) the bench surfaced, and a third (nature) that carves the
executables into their own `exec/` space (§2, axis 3).

== 2. The three orthogonal axes

A module's place is three independent coordinates, not one.

* *Axis 1 — purity (WHO):* a pure model in OSGi space *describes*; host code *actualises* the
  description over the Pulumi engine. No `com.pulumi` / `io.grpc` in OSGi space. This is *one* of the
  top-level splits: `osgi/` vs `host/`.
* *Axis 2 — direction (WHICH WAY):* every module has a northbound face (`Provide` — what it OFFERS, its
  driving side) and a southbound face (`Require` — what it NEEDS, its driven side, toward the infra).
  Direction is NOT a directory split at top level (orientation A, chosen for legibility); it is how a
  module's two manifest faces read, and — inside `host/` — the grouping criterion for adapters.
* *Axis 3 — nature (WHAT KIND):* most modules are *libraries* (a bundle or a plain jar consumed by
  others); a few are *executables* — driving entrypoints with a `main()` that boots the app. Nature is a
  THIRD top-level split, `exec/`, orthogonal to purity: an exec jar is not "host" in the Pulumi/infra
  sense (`netplan-cli` touches zero Pulumi), so grouping executables under `host/` would conflate the
  nature axis with the purity axis. `exec/` groups modules *by what they are* — north-adapters / driving
  entrypoints — not by where they sit on the purity line.

The three axes are independent: a module is (pure-or-host) × (its two manifest faces) × (library-or-
executable). The top-level directory split is purity *and* nature together — `osgi/` and `host/` sort by
purity, `exec/` carves out the executables across both. `bom/` is the lone non-code sibling.

[NOTE]
====
*Why `exec/` is its own space, not a sub-folder of `host/`.* An executable's nature (it drives) is
orthogonal to its purity (whether it touches the engine). `netplan-cli` is a pure-domain driver — a
`main()` over a pure core's `api/` port, zero Pulumi; `seed-master` is a host driver — the composition
root that talks to the engine. Both are *executables* (same nature) but sit on opposite ends of the
purity axis. Putting them together by nature keeps "what kind of thing is this" legible at the top
level; putting them under `host/` would have falsely marked `netplan-cli` as engine-coupled. Each exec
module is a plain `main()` driving a pure core through that core's `api/` package — it is NOT an OSGi
application and launches no framework (the framework lift is the separate runtime-migration phase).
====

In hexagonal terms: a *port* is an interface the core declares (it lives in `osgi/`, as a
`Require`/`Provide-Capability` in the bundle manifest); an *adapter* is an implementation (it lives in
`host/`). The seam = one module's `Require` (south) wired by the resolver to another's `Provide`
(north). There is no third "mediation/bridge" zone — the mediation IS the Provide/Require interplay.

The grid that places everything — purity (columns) crossed with direction (rows), with the driving
*north* now carved out by the nature axis into `exec/`:

[cols="1,2,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| | `osgi/` — core, declares (port) | `host/` — realises (adapter) | `exec/` — drives (nature)

| *north (driving)*
| capability OFFERED (a `Provide` in the bundle manifest)
| —
| the entrypoints: `seed-master` (host driver), `netplan-cli` / `manifests-cli` (pure-domain drivers)

| *south (driven)*
| capability REQUIRED (a `Require` in the bundle manifest) — the "south-ports", e.g. `SectionReader`,
  "write a node"
| `south-adapters` — what actualises toward infra: Pulumi, incus, systemd/dbus, bbox
| —
|===

The driving side is a *nature*, not a host *direction*: the original two-axis sketch placed
"north-adapters" inside `host/`, but an executable's nature is orthogonal to its purity, so step 5 lifted
the drivers out into `exec/` (see §2, axis 3). `host/` is now purely the driven south.

Reads top-to-bottom: callers / the operator drive from the north through the `exec/` entrypoints (the
driving side — `seed-master` and the CLIs), into the pure core's offered capabilities; the core's
required south-ports are wired by the resolver to `host/` `south-adapters`, which actualise down onto
Pulumi at the bottom. "Offer up, depend down." The driving side that was a *direction* (north-adapters)
inside `host/` in the original two-axis sketch is now a *nature* (`exec/`): the bootstrap entry point
moved out of `host/` into `exec/seed-master`, and the CLIs out of their `osgi/` bundles into
`exec/netplan-cli` / `exec/manifests-cli`. What remains in `host/` is purely the driven `south-adapters`.

== 4. Module roster — where every module lands

Coarse-grained on purpose; finer cuts are per-slice cartography (out of scope). Three top-level spaces,
every module in one of them. (`bom` and the `build-parent` / per-space parent poms are the exceptions: a
version catalogue and aggregator/parent scaffolding, not code — they stay top-level siblings, in no
space.)

=== `osgi/` — pure core, declares ports in manifests

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Module | Holds (source today)

| `doctor-core` | the diagnostic record model + clinical vocabulary + the specialist seams (pure part of `controlplane/bdd`)
| `ledger-read` | reconstructs / folds the medical record from history (pure part of `bdd`)
| `config` | `Rke2labConfig`, `InfraDomain`, the Metatype-shaped schema (`controlplane/config`)
| `policy` | `ControlplanePolicy`, `GrantPolicy`, link/debug/provisioning policies — its own module (transverse; `controlplane/policy`)
| `specialist-plugins` | one Unit per specialist (extractable, no back-edge — proven Phase 1)
| `manifests` | the manifests universe + its soldered resolver-adapter (already pure, existing module)
| `unitrepo-core` / `unitrepo-handler-api` | the resolution oracle itself (pure, existing modules)
|===

=== `host/` — adapters, actualise over Pulumi

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Module | Holds (source today)

| `south-adapters` | the driven side: actualisation over Pulumi — `IncusResourceBootstrap` + pipeline + resources + readiness + systemd + bbox (`controlplane/incus,pipeline,resources,readiness,systemd,bbox`), `ledger-write` (per-node serialization, imports `com.pulumi`), `ConfigLoader` (adapts `SectionReader`), and the existing `pulumi-automation-ext` / `pulumi-generated-sdks/incus`
|===

NOTE: the `bdd` package (68 classes) is not a module — it is the interleaving the cartography must
dissolve: its record model + vocabulary + seams → `doctor-core`; its history fold → `ledger-read`; its
per-node write → `ledger-write` (south-adapter); the scenario engine → the driving side now in
`exec/seed-master`. Splitting it is the bulk of a later slice (cartography-first, on fresh bytecode).

=== `exec/` — executables (nature axis), driving entrypoints

Grouped by nature, not purity: each is a `main()` that drives the app over a pure core's `api/` port.
Created in layout step 5 (the netplan/manifests core/CLI splits + `seed-master` moving out of root).

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Module | Holds (source today)

| `seed-master` | the composition root / host driver — `main()` → `ApplicationPipeline.run()` →
  `Pulumi.run(...)`; the deepest north-adapter (talks to the engine). Moved out of repo root.
| `netplan-cli` | a pure-domain driver: `NetplanCli` + commands over `osgi/netplan`'s `api/` port; the
  `-exec` shade jar. Extracted from `osgi/netplan`, which reverted to a pure library bundle.
| `manifests-cli` | a pure-domain driver: `Main` over `osgi/manifests`'s synthesis service; the `-exec`
  shade jar (+ logback). Extracted from `osgi/manifests`, which kept its `manifests-d` asset zip.
|===

NOTE: the three exec jars share their shade flags + filters via `build-parent` (no `exec-parent` — shade
config is global; only per-module `<transformers>` differ: mainClass, logback for the two CLIs,
`Add-Opens` for `seed-master`). `exec/` is an aggregator, not a parent.

=== Technical leaves — placement to confirm per slice

`netplan`, `systemd-contract`, `cdk8s-systemd`, `osgi-bench` are technical/leaf modules. They are pure
(no Pulumi) so they belong in OSGi space by the purity axis, but they carry no Step-2 role; their
physical placement under `osgi/` vs staying top-level is a per-slice detail, not a target decision.

== 5. Scope and method

* *In scope (this doc):* the three spaces, the three axes (purity / direction / nature), the grid, the
  module roster above.
* *Out of scope:* which exact class lands in which module (per-slice cartography on fresh bytecode —
  the `/tmp` jdeps data is ephemeral and was proven stale; re-derive load-bearing coupling claims), the
  adapter sub-grouping inside `south-adapters` (by external system, if one grows too big), and the
  composite-specialist hierarchy (captured, not built).
* *Method:* migrate by MOVE (`git mv`), never duplicate — production code never coexists in two copies.
  Each slice validated by the resolver oracle (a boundary expressed as Provide/Require that fails to
  resolve is a wrong boundary). Build-verify with the FULL `clean package -Posgi
  -Dmaven.build.cache.skipCache=true` (partial `-pl` runs gave false failures from stale `target/`).
