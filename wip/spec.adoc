== Vocabulary

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Term |Meaning
|*Runbook* |the deliverable artifact — a rendered `.adoc` DAG of the scenarios played during a provisioning, with results and (on failure) diagnosis + remediation. Dynamic, on-demand.
|*Readiness checkpoint* |the mechanism — an _intermediate_ controlled passage (a sas between provisioning steps) that plays a Given/When/Then scenario, decides stop-vs-degraded, and feeds the runbook. Today's `SystemdAdapterStage`.
|*Entry gate* |(existing, unchanged) preconditions checked _before_ provisioning — `EntryGatePolicyEnforcer` (clean worktree, flake coherence). A different species of door: the front door, not the intermediate landing.
|*Doctor* |the diagnosis engine the patient consults: Generalist → Specialists → Prescription → RemediationPlan. *Direction (Increment E):* a *generic, pluggable module* (used beyond seed-master), with `Doctor` a supertype of `Generalist`/`Specialist`. See Increment E and its open tensions.
|*Patient* |(Increment E direction) the *Pulumi stack* (`org/project/stack`, e.g. `bioskop/dev`) — a persistent identity. Several patients = several stacks (dev/prod/clusters). A checkpoint is NOT the patient; the patient self-observes _via_ its checkpoints.
|*Visit* |(Increment E direction) one `pulumi up`/`preview` run, carrying its *admission context* = the runtime context (git commit/branch, preview-vs-up mode, call parameters = the Pulumi stack config incl. `simulate`, timestamp).
|*Symptom* |(exists, unchanged) the typed complaint the patient *raises about its own state* while self-observing — the routing key the Generalist uses, never parsed from prose. A consultation carries a *set* of them (0..n).
|*Consultation report* |(Increment E — today's `Dossier`, re-understood) what the patient brings to the doctor + what the doctor produces: status + the raised `Symptom`(s) + summary + details + the `RemediationPlan` (today computed then dropped — to be kept). NOT a separate "exam" layer below it.
|*Medical record* |(Increment E direction) the *patient-stack's* longitudinal history — its visits over time (Pulumi state = the latest; the git-versioned runbook `.adoc` = the longitudinal record). The runbook DAG. One per patient.
|===

The door analogy is deliberate: `EntryGate` is the front door (you pass it to *enter*);
a `ReadinessCheckpoint` is an interior door between rooms (you pass to the next room only
if the current one is in order).

== Overview

We have one readiness checkpoint working: `systemd-adapter` plays a Given/When/Then
scenario during `pulumi up`/`preview`, streams its prose into the Pulumi log, and decides
stop-vs-degraded by severity (see link:../docs/architecture/bdd/bdd-diagnostic-pattern.adoc[bdd-diagnostic-pattern]).

This design extends that into two intertwined subsystems:

. *Runbook* — the deliverable artifact. Not logs: a _runbook of this provisioning_,
  rendered as AsciiDoc, structured as a *directed acyclic graph of scenarios* (the git
  model). It tells the operator what was verified, what passed or failed, and — on failure
  — the diagnosis and the prescribed remediation.
. *Doctor* — the diagnosis engine. When a scenario fails, the checkpoint ("the patient")
  consults a *Generalist*, who triages and routes (deterministically) to *Specialists*,
  who each write a *Prescription* addressed to a remediation program.

*Guiding test:* the runbook is the output, and it is the judge of our analysis. If we
cannot make the runbook contain what an operator needs, the model (checkpoint / doctor /
prescription) is wrong. This is why the design is driven backwards from the artifact.

== Why these analogies

The model was derived from three real-world anchors, not invented:

* *git (DAG):* the runbook is a directed acyclic graph of scenarios. A node is a scenario
  played (dated, with its result + any diagnosis/prescription); an edge is a `dependsOn`.
  Like git commits, nodes are immutable dated facts and several nodes can share an
  ancestor (convergent dependencies — `cluster-ready` and `manifests-applied` both depend
  on `systemd-adapter`). A tree cannot express that sharing; a DAG can. Acyclicity is
  guaranteed because Pulumi already rejects resource cycles.
* *The medical model (doctor):* the patient (checkpoint) detects illness and consults. A
  generalist holds the global view and coordinates; if a first-level diagnosis is
  inconclusive, the generalist refers to specialists, who each prescribe. The generalist
  synthesizes. This maps 1:1 onto failure handling.
* *Real operator runbooks:* validated against two production runbooks, and the K8s-operator
  one shaped the model more than the alerting one — see below.

=== What the K8s-operator runbook taught us (cert-manager)

The Prometheus-operator runbook (Meaning / Impact / Diagnosis / Mitigation) confirmed the
*four-part entry shape*. But it is _static and alert-symptom driven_ — a fixed lookup
table of what could go wrong. The cert-manager troubleshooting guide is closer to our
system and shaped two structural decisions:

. *State-inspection driven, not blind probing.* cert-manager diagnoses by reading each
  resource's live `Status`/`Conditions`/`Events` (`kubectl describe`), not by firing
  commands blindly. Our equivalent: the *snapshot the checkpoint already captured* (target
  state, pending jobs, failed units) IS the Status/Conditions. So the specialist *reads the
  dossier first*, and only probes further if it is inconclusive — the doctor's `firstLook`.
. *Follow the chain of linked resources.* cert-manager troubleshoots by walking
  `Certificate → CertificateRequest → Issuer → Order → Challenge`, reading each. This is
  _literally_ our DAG: when `cluster-ready` fails, the operator walks _up_ the dependency
  edges (`cluster → systemd → incus`) to the root cause. The DAG structure and the nested
  scenarios (`@NestedSteps`) exist to make this walk possible — they were chosen because of
  this anchor, not for their own sake.

== The Runbook

=== Structure: a DAG of scenarios (git model)

[mermaid]
----
graph TB
    subgraph Runbook["Runbook — provisioning 2026-06-05 15:14"]
        CR["cluster becomes ready<br/>✗ DEGRADED"]
        SA["systemd adapter reachable<br/>✓ PASS"]
        IN["incus instance exists<br/>✓ PASS"]
        MA["manifests applied<br/>✓ PASS"]
    end
    CR -->|dependsOn| SA
    MA -->|dependsOn| SA
    SA -->|dependsOn| IN

    style CR fill:#ffe0e0,stroke:#cc0000
    style SA fill:#e0ffe0,stroke:#00aa00
    style IN fill:#e0ffe0,stroke:#00aa00
    style MA fill:#e0ffe0,stroke:#00aa00
----

* *Node* = one scenario played. Carries: scenario id, the Given/When/Then prose, the
  result (pass / fail / degraded), the timestamp, and — on failure — the doctor's
  diagnosis + prescriptions.
* *Edge* = a `dependsOn` relation. `systemd-adapter` appears once even though both
  `cluster-ready` and `manifests-applied` depend on it (convergence the git model handles
  and a tree cannot).
* The rendered `.adoc` is a *view* of this DAG. Like `git log`, it can be traversed
  multiple ways: by execution order, or by walking dependencies _up_ from a failure to its
  root cause (the cert-manager "follow the chain" navigation).

=== Dynamic, not static — fault simulation from day one

A Prometheus runbook is static ("everything that _could_ go wrong"). Ours is *dynamic and
on-demand* — it answers the operator's question _of the moment_:

* After a real `pulumi up`: the runbook of what actually happened, dated.
* During `pulumi preview`: the operator *orders a fake incident* (a probe that fails on
  purpose) and gets the runbook _for that incident_ *without touching live infrastructure*.
  This closes the dry-run convergence we established earlier: _preview = walk the structure,
  emit the doc, no side effects_. The runbook becomes a *fault simulator*: "what happens if
  the dbus adapter is down?" → preview with that fake incident → targeted runbook.

*This is built in Increment A, not deferred.* We want the dynamic runbook-with-incident
immediately — first in its current state, i.e. *with no prescription* (the doctor does not
exist yet). The incident node renders FAILED + the severity decision; the prescription
section fills in once Increment B adds the doctor. Seeing the failed-runbook _before_
building the doctor is the point — it de-risks the runbook's failure rendering first.

==== Ordering a fake incident

The operator orders an incident through a *Pulumi config sub-map*, read with `getObject`,
under a dedicated `policy.preview` namespace — the operator's word for "I'm requesting a
dry-run":

[source,yaml]
----
"rke2lab:policy.preview.simulate":
  systemd-adapter: connection-refused
----

Then `pulumi preview`. For a scenario named in `simulate`, the checkpoint *lifts dry-run
for that scenario* and runs it against a *canned failing probe* (no infra touched). The
fake probe makes it safe; lifting dry-run makes the failure visible in the runbook.
Failure-kind values (`connection-refused`, `timeout`, …) map to typed `Symptom` outcomes.

*Preview-only by construction (safety contract).* The fake-incident map lives in a
`PreviewPolicy`, separate from `ReadinessPolicy.override` (severity), because the two have
different lifecycles: an `override` applies in both preview and apply — it changes how a
_real_ failure is treated — whereas `simulate` is consulted _only_ during preview. The
engine alone decides whether we are previewing (`Deployment.isDryRun()`); the apply path
never reads the simulate map at all. So a stale `simulate` entry left in config can never
inject a fake failure into a real `pulumi up` (which, at CRITICAL severity, would abort an
apply over a defect that does not exist). This is structural, not a guard a future edit can
forget: nothing in `PreviewPolicy` can affect an apply.

[NOTE]
====
This reuses the dry-run plumbing we already have. Normally preview sets
`jgiven.report.dry-run=true` so step bodies are skipped (`deferred-preview`). A simulated
scenario is the exception: its bodies run, but with a fake probe, so the incident appears
without real side effects.
====

=== Each entry has the four runbook parts

Validated against Prometheus + cert-manager. Each scenario node renders as:

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Runbook part |Source in our model
|*Meaning* (why this is verified) |the Given/When/Then prose, plus `@ExtendedDescription` for the _why_
|*Impact* |the scenario's `Severity` (CRITICAL/WARNING) + degraded-mode state
|*Diagnosis* |the doctor reads the captured snapshot first, probes further only if inconclusive
|*Mitigation* |the `Prescription`(s) the doctor produced
|===

On success, only Meaning + Impact render (clean). On failure, Diagnosis + Mitigation
expand — the targeted-capture principle already in the pattern doc.

*Absent Diagnosis/Mitigation is a first-class render state, not a hole.* Until Increment B
the doctor does not exist, so a FAILED node shows Meaning + Impact + FAILED with *no*
Diagnosis/Mitigation. The renderer must treat their absence as a deliberate state (and a
test pins it), so the failure rendering is de-risked before the doctor is wired.

[NOTE]
====
De-risking this first earned its keep: the test (`RunbookRenderingTest`) caught *two* bugs
that would otherwise have rendered an _empty_ runbook on every real failure.

. *`finished()` was skipped on failure.* The checkpoint called JGiven's `finished()` only
  after the `Then` passed, but `finished()` is what flushes the scenario (steps + status)
  into the model. A failing step threw past it, leaving the failed node empty. Fix:
  `finished()` runs in a `finally` (it re-throws the step failure, caught downstream).
. *A standalone-played model has no class name.* JGiven's JUnit runner sets the class name
  the AsciiDoc generator groups scenarios by; the checkpoint plays scenarios standalone
  (raw `Scenario.create()`), so it was null and the report aggregated to _zero_ scenarios.
  Fix: `RunbookRenderer` names the feature before rendering — keeping that detail out of the
  checkpoints and uniform across all of them.
====

*The symptom is sourced, never parsed.* The Diagnosis section names a typed symptom
(`CONNECTION_REFUSED`, …). That value comes from the probe, which classifies its own
failure — it is *not* reverse-engineered from an assertion message. See
<<symptom-and-dossier>>.

Example rendering:

[source,asciidoc]
----
== Scenario: systemd-adapter-becomes-reachable

=== Meaning
The systemd adapter dbus-over-TCP proxy must respond...

=== Diagnosis (on failure only)
**Symptom:** CONNECTION_REFUSED
**Dossier:**
[source,yaml]
----
instanceName: master
units:
  rke2lab-dbus-tcp-system-bus.service:
    status: inactive
...
----
----

=== Collecting the DAG: one shared model, dependencies declared once

The runbook is assembled from a *single shared report model* that lives for the duration of
the provisioning. Today each checkpoint creates a local `new ReportModel()` and discards
it; instead, one model is created at pipeline start, threaded through `PipelineState`, and
each checkpoint records its scenario into it (`addScenarioModel`).

*Edges (dependencies) are not duplicated.* The `dependsOn` fact is _already declared_ at
checkpoint construction — e.g. `SystemdAdapterResource(name, summary, dependsOnResource)`
and the `bootstrapResult.readinessDependency()` passed to every resource. Pulumi consumes
it for ordering but does *not* re-expose it for reading (the SDK exposes
`getChildResources()` parent→child, but no `getDependencies()`). So the same already-known
fact is recorded with the runbook at the moment it is passed to Pulumi: one fact, two
consumers (Pulumi for execution order, the runbook for the narrative DAG). No second source
of truth.

=== Rendering

JGiven 2.0.3 provides the renderers natively (verified in the jar):

* *AsciiDoc:* `AsciiDocReportGenerator` reads a JSON report directory and writes `.adoc`.
  Flow: the shared model is dumped to JSON (`ScenarioJsonWriter`), then the generator
  produces `runbook.adoc`.
* *Inline text:* `PlainTextReporter.toString(ReportModel)` — already used by the checkpoint
  to stream prose into the Pulumi log, pass or fail.

The `.adoc` is rendered at end of provisioning (and at end of preview, including for
ordered fake incidents).

[#render-survives-critical]
==== The render must survive a CRITICAL stop (constraint, not placement)

A CRITICAL scenario stops provisioning by *throwing* `PipelineStageFailure` from inside the
stage (`SystemdAdapterStage.launch()`). If the render were wired at the happy-path tail
(`collectOutputs()` or a terminal `outputs` topic), that throw would unwind _past_ it and
the runbook would never render — in exactly the failure case the runbook exists for.

So the render is *not* an open question of placement; it is a constraint: *the shared model
is created by the caller and rendered in a `finally`*. The caller (`BootstrapStage`, which
already drives the whole `BootstrapPipeline` chain) owns the model, threads it in, and
renders it whether the chain returns or throws:

[source,java]
----
final ReportModel runbook = new ReportModel();   // one instance, owned by the caller
try {
  outputs = BootstrapPipeline.forCluster(config, policy)
      .recordingInto(runbook)                     // threaded via PipelineState into each checkpoint
      … .collectOutputs();
} finally {
  runbookRenderer.render(runbook);                // CRITICAL threw? still renders. degraded? renders.
}
----

The throw stays where it is — aborting resource creation is what CRITICAL _means_ — but it
unwinds _through_ the `finally`. This also satisfies the instance-passing discipline
(CLAUDE.md): one model, created once, passed down the call graph, never re-created per stage
nor reached through a static helper. It replaces today's per-stage `new ReportModel()` that
is built and discarded.

== The Doctor

=== Placement: in the `bdd` package, consulted explicitly by the checkpoint

The doctor lives in the existing `controlplane.bdd` package (with `Severity` and the
stages — one place for the whole BDD model). The checkpoint calls it *explicitly* on
failure: "the patient consults." No magic listener, no nesting inside a ComponentResource.
Package separation is for reuse/readability; the explicit call keeps fidelity to the
medical model.

=== Consultation flow

[mermaid]
----
graph TB
    FAIL["scenario step fails<br/>(the patient is ill)"] --> GEN["Generalist.consult<br/>(snapshot, symptom)"]
    GEN --> FL{"firstLook<br/>conclusive?"}
    FL -->|yes| PLAN["RemediationPlan<br/>(direct)"]
    FL -->|no| ROUTE["deterministic routing<br/>by symptom"]
    ROUTE -->|connection_refused| DBUS["DbusTcpSpecialist"]
    ROUTE -->|connection_refused| NET["NetworkSpecialist"]
    ROUTE -->|instance_not_found| INCUS["IncusExecSpecialist"]
    DBUS --> PLAN
    NET --> PLAN
    INCUS --> PLAN

    style GEN fill:#fff4e6,stroke:#ff9900
    style PLAN fill:#ccffcc,stroke:#00cc66
----

. The checkpoint catches the scenario failure and presents the *dossier* (the snapshot
  already captured: target state, pending jobs, failed units) + the *symptom* (which
  `Then` failed) to the `Generalist`.
. `firstLook` — a first-level diagnosis. If conclusive, the generalist writes the plan
  directly (specialists not disturbed for an obvious case).
. If inconclusive, the generalist routes *deterministically* by symptom to the relevant
  specialists (a readable, testable rules table — no inference "to start"). Irrelevant
  specialists stay dormant.
. Each routed `Specialist` *reads the dossier first* (the snapshot _is_ the
  Status/Conditions, the cert-manager way), probes further only if needed, and writes a
  `Prescription`.
. The generalist synthesizes the prescriptions into a single `RemediationPlan` (its global
  view), which flows into the runbook node and the inline log.

=== Prescription: an action addressed to a remediation program

A prescription is *not* shell commands. It is a corrective action _addressed to a
remediation program_, plus the structured information that program needs. Like a real
prescription: it names a treatment and gives the indications; the specialist writes it, a
remediation program fills it.

* The specialist (or generalist) *knows* the available remediation programs and addresses
  the prescription to one — by a *typed catalog reference*, not a magic string. This
  follows the project's single-source-of-truth discipline (`ManifestDomainCatalog`,
  `SystemdUnitCatalog`); the `clusterApi` bug taught why string addressing fails silently.
* *Today:* the runbook renders the prescription as prose — the operator _is_ the
  remediation program (reads the action + info, acts).
* *Tomorrow:* a real remediation program receives the prescription and executes it. Same
  object, different reader. We do *not* build the executor now (YAGNI the implementation),
  but the model stays open: the prescription carries structured info, never reduced to a
  string.

Rough shape (to be refined in the plan, not frozen here):

[source]
----
RemediationPlan   = { symptom, List<Prescription>, generalistSummary }
Prescription      = { programRef (typed catalog entry), payload (structured info), humanHint }
----

[#symptom-and-dossier]
=== Symptom and dossier: sourced and typed, never parsed

The doctor's two inputs — the symptom (which `Then` failed, and _why_) and the dossier (the
captured state) — must be _data the system already holds_, not values reconstructed at the
catch site. Two decisions enforce this.

*The probe classifies its own failure.* Today the only failure signal is the
`AssertionError` _message_ from the `Then` step. Recovering `CONNECTION_REFUSED` by parsing
that prose is precisely the magic-string fragility the `clusterApi` bug taught us to avoid.
Instead, the probe — which _knows_ connection-refused vs timeout vs binary-missing, that
being its domain — emits a *typed symptom in the envelope on a non-ok result*. The stage
reads `snapshot.get("symptom")`; the Generalist never infers. Fault simulation then falls
out for free: a canned failing probe simply _emits_ `symptom: CONNECTION_REFUSED`, no
special path. *The probe emits the symptom from Increment A* (cheap; nothing routes on it
yet) so the data source is correct from day one; the `Symptom` enum + routing arrive in
Increment B.

*The dossier is the typed successor of the probe envelope — one snapshot, not two.* The
envelope `Map<String,Object>` already _is_ the captured snapshot, and it already flows to
the sink → `SystemdAdapterResource`. Building a separate `Dossier` at the catch site would
revive the two-sources-of-truth anti-pattern. So `Dossier` becomes the record the probe
returns, exposing `toOutputMap()` (the established idiom — cf.
`ControlplanePolicy.toOutputMap()`): one instance, two views — typed for the doctor, a map
for the Pulumi outputs and the YAML block in the runbook.

The retype is *atomic, in Increment B* (no compat shim, per CLAUDE.md): the probe contract
changes from `Map<String,Object>` to `Dossier`, and the sink + `SystemdAdapterResource`
update in the same change. Increment A stays on `Map` (no doctor yet); the typed form lands
exactly when the doctor needs it. `Dossier` is a plain record — serializability (for a
future MCP boundary) follows whenever Phase 2 picks a mechanism, and is not a Phase 1
concern.

== The AI-augmentation seam (Phase 2, not built here)

This system is authored *with* AI (Claude Code), so it should one day be AI-augmentable at
ops-time. We do *not* build that here, and we deliberately do *not* freeze an MCP API we
have not designed — naming specific servers or tool signatures now would be a speculative
abstraction that bit-rots against the real thing. What we _do_ commit to is the *seam*: the
small set of Phase 1 design choices that let a future Phase 2 swap implementations without
rewriting the Generalist, the runbook rendering, or the checkpoints.

*Principle.* Phase 1 is deterministic Java with no AI dependency during bootstrap. The actor
is always the *human operator*; if a future Phase 2 adds AI, the operator invokes it
explicitly for troubleshooting — never the bootstrap itself. The medical analogy simply
extends: the *Doctor* (Generalist) could be an AI agent, *Specialists* could be backed by
out-of-process tools instead of Java classes, *Procedures* could be remote calls instead of
methods — but the contracts between them are the same objects.

=== What the seam is: contracts that don't change

The interface boundary is designed so Phase 1 code survives a Phase 2 that replaces
specialist _implementations_:

[cols="1,2,2",options="header"]
|===
|Contract |Phase 1 |A future Phase 2
|`Specialist.diagnose(Symptom, Dossier)` |Java impl reads the objects in-process |An adapter serializes the dossier, calls an out-of-process tool, deserializes the prescription
|`Symptom` |Java enum (typed, sourced from the probe) |Same enum; its name is the tool's input discriminator
|`Dossier` |plain record, `toOutputMap()` for the YAML view |Same record; serialized when a transport is chosen
|`Prescription` |record: typed `programRef` + structured `payload` + prose `humanHint` |Same record; `humanHint` for humans, `payload`/`programRef` for an executor
|Runbook sections |`== Meaning` / `== Diagnosis` / `== Mitigation`, typed vocabulary |Same headings; structured enough for a machine to parse
|Generalist routing |deterministic symptom → specialist table |unchanged — routes to different _impls_, same table
|===

The `Generalist` does not know whether a `Specialist` is a local Java class or an adapter to
something out-of-process — same interface, swappable implementation. That swappability is the
*entire* Phase 2 surface we are protecting; everything else (transport, tool names, an
executor, a config knob to mix impls) is deferred and intentionally unspecified.

=== Why these specific Phase 1 choices

The seam is the _reason_ for four choices that would otherwise look like over-engineering for
a purely-programmatic doctor:

. *Typed `Symptom`, sourced from the probe* (see <<symptom-and-dossier>>) — not a parsed
  string. A typed discriminator is what any later out-of-process router would key on.
. *`Dossier` as a plain record with `toOutputMap()`* — one snapshot, two views. Serializable
  later by whatever mechanism Phase 2 picks; no serialization dependency pulled in now.
. *`Prescription` with a structured `payload` beside the prose `humanHint`* — the operator
  reads the hint today; a structured payload is what an executor would consume later. The
  prescription is never reduced to a string.
. *Structured runbook headings + typed vocabulary* — the `== Diagnosis`/`== Mitigation`
  structure and names like `CONNECTION_REFUSED` are deterministic anchors a machine could
  parse, without us designing that parser now.

== Implementation: one design, three sequenced increments

Think global (this design), deliver incremental. Each increment ships green.

=== Increment A — Runbook (render + fake incident) — DONE

Build the runbook mechanism on the *existing* systemd-adapter checkpoint.

* *Shared report model, caller-owned (R1).* `BootstrapStage` creates one `ReportModel`,
  threads it through `PipelineState` (via `recordingInto`) into each checkpoint (replacing
  today's per-stage `new ReportModel()` that is built and discarded), and renders it in a
  `finally` so a CRITICAL throw still produces a runbook.
* Render the runbook (`AsciiDocReportGenerator`, in that `finally`) alongside the existing
  inline text. The generator emits a *directory* — `index.asciidoc` (entry) + `features/`
  + statistics — not a single file; `RunbookRenderer` owns this and renders to
  `target/runbook/adoc/`.
* *Probe emits the symptom (R2).* On a non-ok result the probe envelope carries a typed
  `Symptom`. Nothing routes on it yet (the doctor is absent), but the data source is correct
  from day one and fault simulation reuses it directly.
* *Fake-incident ordering, preview-only:* `policy.preview.simulate.<scenario>:
  <failure-kind>` config sub-map in a dedicated `PreviewPolicy` (separate from
  `ReadinessPolicy.override`); a simulated scenario lifts dry-run and runs a canned failing
  probe (which emits the symptom). Renders a FAILED node + severity decision — *no
  prescription yet* (doctor absent). Consulted only in preview (see <<symptom-and-dossier>>'s
  sibling safety note above).
* *Absent Diagnosis/Mitigation is a tested state (R6).* `RunbookRenderingTest` pins that a
  FAILED node renders cleanly with those sections absent (not a null hole). Doing this first
  caught two empty-runbook bugs (skipped `finished()`; null class name) — see the note under
  "the four runbook parts".
* *Deferred to Increment C:* recording `dependsOn` as DAG edges — there is only one

=== Increment E — the medical model, refined (design; execution in link:plan.adoc[plan.adoc])

A terminology session (2026-06-07) turned the medical analogy into a precise *layered* model
and resolved that the "shape the snapshot/outputs first" work and the "build the DAG" work are
*not two chantiers* — they are *layers of one model*. This supersedes the flat "everything is a
`Dossier`" granularity used through Increment D.

NOTE: This section captures a *direction*, deliberately NOT frozen. The model moved a lot during
the session (patient went from "the provisioned system" → "the checkpoint" → finally "the Pulumi
stack"; the Doctor became a generic module). Rather than freeze a moving target, we record the
direction + the open tensions, and we will only settle them once a *first working version of the
doctor(s)* is in place (the user's call). The concrete *next executable step* (layer 2 below) is
stable and does not depend on the unsettled tensions.

==== The patient is the Pulumi stack; it self-observes and consults about its symptoms

The flow is *patient → doctor*, not doctor → patient. The patient self-observes, *raises its
own symptoms* about its state, and *comes to consult* the doctor about them. The doctor measures
nothing; it receives the raised symptoms and diagnoses/prescribes.

* *Patient = the Pulumi stack* (`org/project/stack`, e.g. `bioskop/dev`) — a *persistent*
  identity. Several patients = several stacks (dev/prod, different clusters). (We tried
  "patient = the provisioned system", then "patient = the checkpoint"; both were too poor. The
  stack is the entity with a durable identity and a history.)
* *A visit* = one `pulumi up`/`preview`. It carries the *admission context* = the runtime
  context: git commit/branch (already captured in `OutputBuilder` `seedDeploymentMetadata.git`),
  preview-vs-up mode (`Deployment.getInstance().isDryRun()`), the *call parameters* = the Pulumi
  stack config (incl. `policy.preview.simulate.<scenario>`, the ordered fake incident), and a
  timestamp. Much of this is already assembled in `OutputBuilder` but does **not** reach the
  runbook today — wiring it in is part of this increment.
* *A checkpoint is where the patient self-observes* one subsystem (systemd-adapter, cluster, …)
  and raises symptoms. It is NOT a patient, and NOT something the doctor examines.
* *The medical record* = the patient-stack's longitudinal history of visits. Pulumi state holds
  only the *latest* visit; the *git-versioned runbook `.adoc`* is the longitudinal record.

*No separate "exam"/"observation" layer.* An earlier draft put an `ExamReport`/`Observation` type
below the consultation; that was one abstraction too many. There are two model types only: the
`Symptom` the patient raises, and the *consultation report* it brings to the doctor (today's
`Dossier`, re-understood — see below). The runbook DAG is the third, the medical record.

==== A consultation carries a SET of symptoms, bounded by observability

The patient self-observes across its checkpoint's points (the cluster's three phases:
kubeconfig → API → controllers) and raises *all* the symptoms it finds — a consultation carries a
*set* (0..n), and the doctor sees the set (so it can correlate). BUT how many a given patient can
actually raise is bounded by *observability*:

* *Independent observations* (a future pair of unrelated checks) → all are observable → the full
  set is raised.
* *A precondition chain* (the cluster today: you cannot reach the API without the kubeconfig, nor
  the controllers without the API) → downstream points are *physically unobservable* once an
  upstream one fails → the set collected is bounded at the first blocking symptom.

This is exactly the user's "the edge decides": fail-fast *along* a precondition chain (downstream
not observable), all-together *between* independent observations. The cluster therefore raises ≈1
symptom today; the `Set<Symptom>` shape is ready for more when an independent pair appears.

==== The Doctor is a generic, pluggable module (design module-ready now; extract later)

The user's call: the Doctor must be *an actor of the system — a generic module plugged into
seed-master, reusable in other contexts*. This settles the long-open question recorded in
link:../.claude/memory/config-restructuring-state.md[config-restructuring-state] /
link:../.claude/memory/domain-registry-abstraction.md[domain-registry-abstraction]: the doctor
core is a *neutral module*, not seed-master-bound. Rule of three is reached — (1) runbook
checkpoints, (2) config missing-input remediation (config's "Increment 2", the doctor's first
non-runbook use case), (3) future contexts.

* *Decision:* design layers 2/3 *module-ready* now — the doctor core must know *no concrete
  symptom or context*. Physical extraction into a dedicated Maven module + wiring config to it is
  a *separate, later* chantier (churn controlled).
* *What "module-ready" forces (deeper than it first looks):* not only must `Symptom` and
  `SpecialistDomain` become *open* (each context contributes its own — today they are closed
  enums mixing systemd + cluster symptoms), but `Generalist.routeBySymptom()` is a *hard-coded
  closed `switch` inside the core*. A generic doctor cannot contain that switch — the
  *symptom→domain routing must itself be contributed data*, not a core method. This is the real
  extraction work.
* *How the module integrates with Pulumi (RESOLVED 2026-06-07).* The doctor is NOT a Pulumi
  resource — resources are *provisioned* toward a desired state, but a doctor is behaviour and the
  patient is the stack (modelling either as a resource walks on Pulumi's toes). The doctor stays
  *application logic*; only the *consultation report* (the result) is carried into the state as
  `registerOutputs` on the checkpoint's existing `ComponentResource` (Option A) — an output-carrier,
  not a provisioned actor. Provider-backed CustomResource (Option B) is rejected: re-observation
  already happens in-program (the probes re-run each up), so a provider `Read` reconstructs managed
  external state we don't have. Full argument + C4/UML diagrams in
  link:pulumi-doctor-integration.adoc[pulumi-doctor-integration.adoc]. (A Pulumi-*domain* Specialist
  — an expert reading engine errors — remains possible later as just another `Specialist`, distinct
  from this persistence question.)

==== Layer 3 — the medical record (the deferred DAG chantier; decisions LOCKED, code in a fresh session)

The medical record is the patient-stack's aggregate of its visit's consultations + admission
context. Decisions locked 2026-06-07 (to be CODED in a fresh session — this conversation is far past
its context budget and serialization + the phantom-diff constraint deserve a clean start):

* *Our model is the SOURCE; runbook and `VerificationResult` are VIEWS.* The medical record is OUR
  aggregate (nodes = checkpoints, explicit `dependsOn` edges, admission context, each node's
  dossiers + plan). This *reverses today's dependency*: currently the JGiven `ReportModel` IS the
  structure and the runbook renders from it; instead our model-record becomes the source and the
  JGiven `ReportModel` is *derived from it* for rendering. JGiven becomes one render backend, not the
  structure owner. `VerificationResult` likewise becomes a view computed from the record's cluster
  node. This resolves the renderer fork toward "our own renderer over our model" (JGiven still usable
  as a backend).
* *Runtime = in-memory; state = its serialization; post-hoc = reconstruction.* Two moments that were
  conflated: (1) *during a run* the program gets no graph back and checkpoints play eager (before
  downstream registers), so the record MUST be an in-memory model — you cannot read it from the
  not-yet-written state; (2) *post-hoc* it is reconstructed from the persisted state.
* *Full serialization into state (user's call): the WHOLE record — plans included — is written to
  the outputs* so it reconstructs 100% faithfully from the state alone (not just the skeleton). This
  is "the medical record IS the Pulumi state" in the strong sense.

*HARD CONSEQUENCE — the byte-identical guard is RENEGOTIATED, not just respected.* Writing the full
record adds output keys, which contradicts the "no output changes" stance held all session. The
redefined guard:

* *Unchanged (the Stage-B contract an EXTERNAL orchestrator reads):* `handoffReady`, `nextStep`,
  `bootstrapStatus`, the 7 `cluster*` keys, the systemd flat keys.
* *Added (additive — does not break existing readers):* a new key (e.g. `medicalRecord`) carrying the
  serialized record.

*HARD PROBLEM to solve FIRST when coding — the phantom diff.* `OutputBuilder` *deliberately omits*
`timestamp` today with the comment "it would change every run and produce phantom output diffs on
no-op pulumi up". The full record contains admission (timestamp, git commit) and possibly varying
plans → serializing it re-introduces exactly that phantom diff (every `up`, even a no-op, shows an
output diff; the operator can't tell a real change from a clock tick). Parades (exclude timestamp
from the serialized blob, or a stable hash) tension with "100% faithful reconstruction" — if the
timestamp is excluded to avoid the phantom diff, it is no longer 100% faithful. Resolve this
trade-off at the top of the layer-3 work.

==== Layer 2 — DONE (commit 0cd2b2f5): the doctor's plan is kept, not dropped

Shipped: `ConsultationReport` (the raised `Dossier`s + the `RemediationPlan` the doctor wrote) +
a caller-owned `ConsultationLog`, threaded through the pipeline exactly like the runbook
`ReportModel` (`recordingInto(runbook, consultations)` → `PipelineState` → both checkpoint stages).
On a raised symptom each stage records a `ConsultationReport` into the shared log — so the plan,
previously computed-logged-then-discarded in `consultDoctor(...)`, is now kept. Applied uniformly to
BOTH stages. In-memory only; Pulumi outputs untouched (the byte-identical guard held;
`ClusterReadinessProjectionTest` unchanged). 35/35 green.

*Scope corrections found while implementing (the plan was written before the reactive model):*

* `Dossier` is NOT renamed to `ExamReport` — there is no exam layer (resolved earlier).
* `Set<Symptom>` was NOT built — the cluster phases are a fail-fast precondition chain, so the set
  is always size 1 today; building it now is the rule-of-three speculation we deferred. The report
  already carries *all* phase dossiers; a multi-symptom shape waits for a genuine independent pair.
* "`VerificationResult` becomes a projection of the *consultation report*" turned out to be
  *impossible AND already-half-done*: a healthy checkpoint raises no symptom → produces NO
  consultation report, yet a `VerificationResult` always exists (success/fail/skipped/deferred).
  They are TWO distinct aggregates — `VerificationResult` = the always-present *Stage-B handoff*
  projection (already a projection of the phase dossiers since Increment D); `ConsultationReport` =
  the *medical* data, only on a symptom. They must NOT be merged. The DAG (layer 3) consumes the
  consultation reports, never the `VerificationResult`. So the old "unify the output shape" goal of
  layer 2 dissolved — it was layer 3 in disguise (making `VerificationResult` a *view of the medical
  record* IS building the medical record). The only real residual dup — the 7 `cluster*` keys defined
  twice (`VerificationResult.asOutputs()` + `ReadinessOutputMapper.OUTPUT_EXTRACTORS`) — is a local
  handoff-side cleanup, optional, unrelated to the medical model.

==== Settled during the session (no longer open)

* *T1 — when is the doctor consulted?* RESOLVED: *reactively, on a symptom* (as the code does
  today — `consultDoctor` only in the failure branch), NOT systematically per visit. The
  root-cause correlation that a systematic end-of-visit consultation would give is obtained
  instead via edge-propagated record access (T2). Systematic consultation is inappropriate: it
  would run even when fail-fast wants to stop, and decouple the diagnosis from the symptom's
  fresh context.
* *Flow + terminology.* RESOLVED: patient → doctor (the patient self-observes and raises
  symptoms); no `ExamReport`/`Observation` layer; today's `Dossier` *is* the consultation report.

==== Open tensions — DEFERRED until a first doctor(s) version is in place

Recorded, not resolved (user: "note all the tensions; tackle them once our first version
implementing the doctor(s) is in place"):

. *Stateless doctor vs panel.* If the Doctor is a shared module, is record access an *internal
  state* of the doctor (a panel of patients) or a *parameter passed to each* `consult()` (the
  record + its `dependsOn` chain)? The latter fits a stateless module and the "access propagated
  along edges" idea; it tensions with "the doctor has a panel".
. *Routing must be contributed.* (See module-ready above.) `routeBySymptom` hard-coded in the core
  contradicts genericity — routing becomes contributed data. Deeper than opening the enums.
. *Context: patient vs visit.* The persistent patient identity is only `org/project/stack`; the
  runtime context (git, mode, `simulate`, timestamp) is the admission of *a visit*, not the
  patient's identity. Decides where the data attaches in the types.

==== Renderer-route fork (decide before coding layer 3)

The runbook is rendered by JGiven's own `AsciiDocReportGenerator` over a `ReportModel` — it is
_not_ a free-form template. Adding node-level Diagnosis/Mitigation forces a choice: (a) attach the
plan into the JGiven model (InfoTags / attachments / extended description — stays in JGiven,
JGiven owns layout); (b) replace `AsciiDocReportGenerator` with our own renderer over
`ReportModel` (full control, more work); (c) hybrid (JGiven body + our appended node sections).
`RunbookRenderingTest` asserts "no Diagnosis/Mitigation before the doctor exists" — flipping that
assertion is the TDD entry point.
